# 5.3 Loss-based bandwidth estimation

Section 5, *Bandwidth, congestion and unstable links*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Unknown or changing available bandwidth -> GCC: loss-based estimation

## The issue

Delay is not the only congestion signal. Some links drop packets without first building a queue: shallow buffers, policers, wireless links with random loss. Others have so much competing traffic that queue delay never stabilizes. If the sender ignores loss, it can keep sending into a link that discards a large share of its packets.

Not every loss means congestion, though: wireless links lose packets randomly at any rate. A controller that backs off on every loss starves itself on a lossy-but-fast link. It must distinguish light loss (ignore), moderate loss (hold) and heavy loss (back off), using enough packets that the measured loss rate means something.

## Design

Applied to the current target rate A once per measurement window:

```
  loss < 2%   no loss-based limit (the delay-based estimate governs)
  2% - 10%    hold: limit = A
  loss > 10%  decrease: limit = A * (1 - 0.5 * loss), at most once per
              300 ms + RTT
```

Loss is measured over windows of at least 1 s and 20 packets, so a lucky or unlucky handful of packets cannot swing the estimate. (libwebrtc v1 additionally caps increases at 8%/s after start-up; here the delay-based controller and probing own the upward direction.)

libwebrtc's LossBasedBweV2 (`cc v2`), ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/congestion_controller/goog_cc/loss_based_bwe_v2.{h,cc} (M157) with the constants and defaults of CreateConfig (no field trial set), and the target state machine of SendSideBandwidthEstimation around it (send_side_bandwidth_estimation.{h,cc}).

LossBasedBweV2 keeps a window of 15 observations (each at least 250 ms of sending: packets, bytes, bytes lost, sending rate). It fits the channel model

```
  p(loss) = q + (1 - q) * (R - B) / R     when R > B, else q
```

(q the inherent loss, B the loss-limited bandwidth, R the sending rate) to the window by maximum likelihood, one Newton step on q per candidate bandwidth (current x {1.02, 1.0, 0.95}, the acknowledged rate, a higher delay-based estimate), and picks the candidate with the highest objective, which includes a bias toward higher bandwidth when the average loss is under 20 %. The result is bounded by the instant upper bound (100 kbps / (loss - 5 %)), the acked rate and the delay-based estimate, held (HOLD, 300 ms doubling to 60 s) after a decrease, and ramped up at most 1.3 x per 300 ms and 1.5 x the acked rate.

A packet counts as lost only when a feedback message covers it and reports it as not received (PacketResult::received() false); a packet that a later feedback reports as received is removed from the lost set again.

libwebrtc's SendSideBandwidthEstimation (`cc v2`), ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/congestion_controller/goog_cc/ send_side_bandwidth_estimation.{h,cc} (M157), with its constants and defaults.

It owns the target rate: the delay-based estimate caps it, the loss-based estimator (LossBasedBweV2, cc/loss_based_bwe_v2.h) sets it once it has three observations, the RTT backoff (3 s limit, x0.8 per second, 5 kbps floor) cuts it when feedback stops, and the receiver limit (REMB) caps what target_rate() returns. Before the loss-based estimator is ready it uses the receiver-report loss rule of the original GCC (UpdatePacketsLost: below 2 % +8 % of the minimum of the last second, 2-10 % hold, above 10 % x(1 - loss/2)), and during the first two seconds after the first loss report it trusts the delay-based estimate.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#5-congestion-control) for the differences from libwebrtc. Deviation from libwebrtc v1: below 2% loss this controller does not limit at all (delay-based and probing own the upward direction), whereas libwebrtc caps increases at 8%/s after start-up. `cc v2` (#12) is libwebrtc's code instead: `LossBasedBweV2` (`loss_based_bwe_v2.h`, CreateConfig's defaults) fits the loss model p = q + (1 - q)(R - B)/R to 15 observations of at least 250 ms (byte loss rate, weights 0.9^age) by one Newton step per candidate bandwidth (current x 1.02 / 1.0 / 0.95, the acked rate, a higher delay-based estimate), bounds the result by the instant upper bound 100 kbps / (loss - 5%) and the acked rate, holds after a decrease (300 ms doubling to 60 s), limits increases to 1.3x per 300 ms and 1.5x the acked rate, and reports the state the probe controller reads. A packet counts as lost only when a feedback covers it and reports it not received; a later report as received removes it again. `SendSideBandwidthEstimation` (`send_side_bandwidth_estimation.h`) owns the target: it takes the loss-based result once three observations exist, trusts the delay-based estimate in the first 2 s after the first receiver report, falls back to the receiver-report loss rule before that, and cuts x0.8 per second when the propagation RTT is above 3 s. Remaining differences: the report-block loss deltas come from `rtp::Session` (not libwebrtc's `RtpTransportControllerSend`, which is not in `reference/`, #57), no field trials, and `ProbeController` reads the loss-based state but not `LossBasedState::kIncreaseUsingPadding` padding (the pacer sends probes, not loss-based padding).

## Verification notes

Defect found: loss was measured over 20-packet intervals, and a lucky interval with no loss (about 4% likely at 15% loss) let the estimate jump from 153 to 2 338 kbps. Now loss windows are at least 1 s and decisions are applied to the current target. `cc v2`: the unit tests port libwebrtc's `loss_based_bwe_v2_test.cc` cases with their numbers; the scenarios `issue_12_lossbwe_*` check 5% random loss (utilization 76-82% against libwebrtc's 76%), burst loss, a policer, a shallow buffer and congestive loss, and a lossless jittery link.
