# 5.2 Delay-based bandwidth estimation (GCC)

Section 5, *Bandwidth, congestion and unstable links*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Unknown or changing available bandwidth -> GCC: delay-based estimation

## The issue

Nobody tells the sender how much bandwidth is available, and it changes during a call (cross traffic, Wi-Fi rate changes, handovers). Sending too slowly wastes quality. Sending too fast fills router queues: delay grows by hundreds of milliseconds (bufferbloat) long before packets are dropped, and by the time loss appears the call is already lagging. Loss-based TCP-style control therefore reacts too late for real-time media.

Rising one-way delay is the early signal, but it is buried in noise (jitter, burst sending, clock offset). A controller must extract the trend, decide overuse versus normal versus underuse without overreacting to single spikes, and adjust the rate: probing upward carefully, backing off decisively but not repeatedly for the same event.

## Design

Pipeline, per transport feedback:

```
  InterArrival        groups packets sent within 5 ms (a burst) and yields
                      per-group send/receive deltas.
  TrendlineEstimator  accumulates the one-way delay variation
                      (recv_delta - send_delta), smooths it, and fits a
                      least-squares slope over the last 20 groups. A rising
                      queue shows up as a positive slope.
  Overuse detection   compares the scaled slope against an adaptive
                      threshold (which grows slowly toward |trend| so
                      competing TCP flows do not starve us).
  AimdRateController  increase (multiplicative far from the link capacity
                      estimate, additive near it), hold, or decrease to
                      0.85 x the acknowledged throughput on overuse.
  AcknowledgedBitrate throughput actually delivered, used to anchor
                      decreases and cap increases.
```

libwebrtc's acknowledged-bitrate and AIMD rate control (`cc v2`), ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/congestion_controller/goog_cc/ {bitrate_estimator,acknowledged_bitrate_estimator,link_capacity_estimator}.{h,cc} and modules/remote_bitrate_estimator/aimd_rate_control.{h,cc} (M157), with their constants and defaults (no field trials set).

```
  BitrateEstimator    Bayesian estimate of the delivered rate: 500 ms first
                      window, then 150 ms windows, sample uncertainty
                      scale 10.
  LinkCapacityEstimator  EWMA (alpha 0.05 on overuse) of the acked rate at
                      overuse, with a normalised deviation in [0.4, 2.5].
  AimdRateControl     send-side AimdRateControl: hold/increase/decrease,
                      beta 0.85, reduce again only after clamp(rtt, 10,
                      200) ms, increase limited to 1.5 x acked + 10 kbps.
```

The ALR state (cc/alr_detector.h, #48) reaches the estimator as in libwebrtc: SetAlr and, when the region ends, SetAlrEndedTime, after which the first packet sent later calls ExpectFastRateChange.

Differences that remain (see tools/catalog.py 5.2): PacketResult has no prior_unacked_data (always zero here); no NetworkStateEstimate; SetInApplicationLimitedRegion of the rate control only matters with a field trial that is off by default.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#5-congestion-control) for the differences from libwebrtc. After a capacity increase, GCC climbs multiplicatively at 8%/s. Going from 650 to 2500 kbps takes about 17 s, so the last phase has a lower utilization bar. `cc v2` (`GccConfig::version`, issue #19) starts the port of libwebrtc's GoogCC as a unit (#61): the detector's front end is `DelayBasedBwe::IncomingPacketFeedbackVector` with `InterArrivalDelta` (`BelongsToBurst`, reordered groups, feedback sorted by receive time, 2 s stream time-out). The trendline detector was already libwebrtc's. #15 adds the rest of the overuse path to `cc v2`: the acknowledged rate (#15: `BitrateEstimator`, 500 ms first window, then 150 ms Bayesian windows; since #150 `RobustThroughputEstimator`, see below), `AimdRateControl` (`aimd_rate_control.h`: near-max additive increase, decrease with `beta x throughput - 5 kbps`, `LinkCapacityEstimator`, `TimeToReduceFurther`, `bitrate_is_initialized_`) and `DelayBasedBwe::MaybeUpdateEstimate` (halve every `clamp(rtt, 10, 200)` ms when overusing without an acked rate; a probe result sets the estimate as is). #48 adds `AlrDetector` (`alr_detector.h`: the ALR state marks the acked-rate estimator, and the end of an ALR calls `ExpectFastRateChange`), the probe controller and probe estimator (5.6), and `recovered_from_overuse`, which requests a probe after a big drop. #166 ports `delay_based_bwe_unittest.cc` (20 cases, `DelayBweLibwebrtc`; found that `Result.updated` is `ValidEstimate()`, not always true) and `robust_throughput_estimator_unittest.cc`; `acknowledged_bitrate_estimator_unittest.cc` (3 cases, `AcknowledgedBitrateEstimator`, with a recording `BitrateEstimator` in place of the gmock) follows. M157's acknowledged-bitrate estimator is `RobustThroughputEstimator` (`RobustThroughputEstimatorSettings::enabled = true`, `AcknowledgedBitrateEstimatorInterface::Create`), and since #150 `cc v2` feeds it with the receive-sorted feedback instead of the `BitrateEstimator` (the ALR marks are no-ops there, as in libwebrtc). Not ported, because we use transport-cc with send-side estimation: the receive-side `remote_bitrate_estimator_*`, `inter_arrival`, `overuse_detector` and `congestion_control_feedback_*` tests. Remaining differences: `prior_unacked_data` is zero, no `NetworkStateEstimate`.

## Verification notes

Defects found. (1) The AIMD controller reduced the rate on every feedback while overuse lasted (4 cuts in 500 ms); a libwebrtc-style guard now allows one decrease per RTT unless throughput collapses. (2) The simulator's periodic 3x keyframes every 3 s triggered regular overuse and held utilization near 50%; real WebRTC sends keyframes on request, and so does the simulator now. Utilization rose to ~80%, with queue p95 of 20-33 ms.
