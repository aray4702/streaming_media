# 5.5 Pacer with priority queues

Section 5, *Bandwidth, congestion and unstable links*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Bursts filling router queues (bufferbloat) -> Pacer: smooths packet sends, with priority queues
- Protecting audio over video -> audio prioritized in the pacer; video degrades first

## The issue

Encoders produce a whole frame at once, and a keyframe can be tens of kilobytes. Writing it to the socket instantly sends a line-rate burst that briefly exceeds the bottleneck's capacity. Router queues fill (adding delay for everything behind the burst), packets are dropped, and the congestion controller sees self-inflicted congestion and lowers the rate for no good reason.

Audio packets queued behind a video burst arrive late, which is worse because audio dropouts hurt more than video stutter. Retransmissions are also time-critical. Packets must be spread out at a controlled rate, important traffic must go first, and the queue must never grow so large that video is seconds old when it is sent.

## Design

Encoders emit a whole frame at once; sending it as a line-rate burst fills router queues (bufferbloat) and causes self-inflicted loss. The pacer releases packets at the pacing rate (usually 2.5 x the target) and queues them per priority so audio is never stuck behind a keyframe and video degrades first. Probe clusters are sent at their own rate and tagged so the probe estimator can find them; padding fills a probe (or a requested padding rate) when there is no media to send.

The pacer is libwebrtc's PacingController and PrioritizedPacketQueue, ported as they are (cc/pacing_controller.h, issue #70), run behind TaskQueuePacedSender's scheduling. The library's own leaky-bucket pacer (`pacer v1`) was deleted in #167.

A port of modules/pacing/pacing_controller.cc, prioritized_packet_queue.cc (M157, compare/webrtc/reference/), behind a `Timestamp now` API with no task queue. Encoders emit a whole frame at once; sending it as a line-rate burst fills router queues (bufferbloat) and causes self-inflicted loss. The pacer releases packets at the pacing rate (usually 2.5 x the target) using libwebrtc's leaky bucket:

```
  - `media_debt` grows with every packet sent and drains at the (adjusted)
    pacing rate; packets may leave in a burst of `send_burst_interval`
    (40 ms) at a time, and the debt is capped at 500 ms of the rate;
  - the time that is credited since the last send is capped at 2 s;
  - a queue that cannot drain within `queue_time_limit` (2 s, judged on the
    average queue time, not the oldest packet) raises the rate;
  - the first packet of a keyframe flushes the queued packets of the video
    stream (video, FEC, retransmissions), as EnqueuePacket does;
  - padding fills a requested padding rate, 5 ms of it at a time.
```

Packets are queued per priority level, as PrioritizedPacketQueue does:

```
  0 audio, 1 retransmission, 3 video and FEC (one FIFO), 4 padding
```

(level 2, video retransmission, is only used when audio retransmission is prioritised, which libwebrtc does not do by default). Audio is not paced (it leaves at its enqueue time) but is counted in the debt, as a call with audio in the congestion-control allocation does. Probe clusters are sent at their own rate and tagged so the probe estimator can find them (#48): the BitrateProber times each burst by the cluster rate, a burst starts with a small padding packet, and padding fills a burst when there is no media.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#5-congestion-control) for the differences from libwebrtc. The pacer is a port of libwebrtc's PacingController and PrioritizedPacketQueue (M157, issue #70); the library's own media-debt pacer (`pacer v1`: 5 ms burst, drain on the oldest packet) was deleted in #167. It has a 40 ms burst interval (capped at 63 kB), media debt capped at 500 ms of the rate, 2 s cap on the time credited, a queue-time limit of 2 s judged on the average queue time, audio unpaced but accounted, queue levels audio / retransmission / video+FEC / padding, keep-alive padding every 500 ms while paused or congested, and padding at the padding rate 5 ms at a time. The first packet of a keyframe flushes the queued video, FEC and retransmission packets, as `PacingController::EnqueuePacket` does (the sender marks keyframes and first-of-frame packets; #5). With RTX the padding is made by `RtpSender::GeneratePadding` (`PacketRouter::GeneratePadding`): RTX payload padding from the history first, then 255-byte empty padding packets on the RTX SSRC, each with its real size in the pacer queue; the keep-alive and the first packet of a probe ask for 1 byte (#120). Without it the pacer queues 224 B placeholders. Remaining differences: round-robin between SSRCs of one level (one stream per kind in a one-to-one call), the packet TTLs (off by default), `SetCongested` is not driven by the congestion window (pushback stays on the encoder). `BitrateProber` (#48, `bitrate_prober.h`) times the probe bursts: a cluster starts when a media packet of at least min(`rate x min_probe_delta`, 200 B) is enqueued, begins with a small padding packet, sends `rate x min_probe_delta` bytes per burst (padding fills it when there is no media), times the next burst by the bytes sent at the cluster rate and drops a cluster whose burst is more than 10 ms late (at most 5 pending, 5 s old). The pacer is also scheduled as `TaskQueuePacedSender` does (`Pacer::ProcessScheduled`, #123): a pass runs only when a packet, a rate or the pause state changed or the timer of the last pass is due, and only while `NextSendTime()` has come, with 5 ms (3 packets) between timer passes. Polling a bare `PacingController` every millisecond lets the early-return branch of `ProcessPackets` re-base its debt every call and sends queued packets between the audio packets instead of with them.

```cpp
namespace sm::cc {

struct PacerConfig {
  // Wire size of one padding packet.
  DataSize padding_packet = DataSize::Bytes(224);
  EventSink* events = nullptr;
  // See PacingControllerConfig; its padding_packet and events are taken from above.
  PacingControllerConfig controller;
  // TaskQueuePacedSender's scheduling around the PacingController. It runs a pass only
  // when a packet, a rate or the pause state changed, or when a timer set by the last pass is
  // due, and only if NextSendTime() has come; consecutive timer passes are at least
  // `hold_back_window` apart. Without it every Process() call is a pass, and the early-return
  // branch of ProcessPackets then re-bases the leaky bucket on every call.
  bool task_queue_scheduling = true;
  TimeDelta hold_back_window = TimeDelta::Millis(5);  // RtpTransportControllerSend: 5 ms, 3 packets.
  int hold_back_packets = 3;
};
```

```cpp
namespace sm::cc {

struct PacingControllerConfig {
  // PacingController::Configuration defaults.
  TimeDelta queue_time_limit = TimeDelta::Millis(2000);  // kMaxExpectedQueueLength
  bool drain_large_queues = true;
  TimeDelta send_burst_interval = TimeDelta::Millis(40);  // PacerConfig::kDefaultTimeInterval
  // Audio waits for the budget only with WebRTC-Pacer-BlockAudio (off by default).
  bool pace_audio = false;
  // AccountForAudioPacketsInPacedSender(true), what AudioSendStream does when audio is
  // part of the congestion-control allocation.
  bool account_for_audio = true;
  // Wire size of one padding packet (RTPSender::GeneratePadding, kMaxPaddingLength).
  DataSize padding_packet = DataSize::Bytes(224);
  // Wire size of the small padding packet that starts every probe burst
  // (GeneratePadding(1 byte): the RTP sender pads at least 50 bytes; plus the
  // 12-byte RTP header, the header extensions and the 28 bytes of UDP/IPv4).
  DataSize probe_start_padding = DataSize::Bytes(102);
  BitrateProberConfig prober;
  int circuit_breaker_iterations = 1 << 16;
  EventSink* events = nullptr;
};

// PrioritizedPacketQueue (modules/pacing/prioritized_packet_queue.cc, M157),
// the pacer's queue. One FIFO per priority level, as GetPriorityForType sets
// them: 0 audio, 1 retransmission, 3 video and FEC (one FIFO), 4 padding.
// Level 2 (video retransmission) is used only with
// prioritize_audio_retransmission, which is off by default, and the
// time-to-live per level (PacketQueueTTL) is infinite by default, so neither
// is ported. A one-to-one call has one stream of each kind, so the round robin
// between the SSRCs of a level is not needed either. Sizes are the packets'
// wire sizes (libwebrtc's SizeInPayloadBytes counts payload and padding only).
// Enqueue times are kept minus the time the queue has spent paused, so that
// AverageQueueTime counts only the time spent unpaused.
class PrioritizedPacketQueue {
 public:
  static constexpr int kNumPriorityLevels = 5;

  void Push(Timestamp enqueue_time, PacedPacket p);
  // The first packet of the highest-priority level; nullopt when empty.
  std::optional<PacedPacket> Pop();
  size_t SizeInPackets() const { return size_packets_; }
  DataSize SizeInBytes() const { return size_bytes_; }
  bool Empty() const { return size_packets_ == 0; }
  // Queued packets per PacketPriority (SizeInPacketsPerRtpPacketMediaType).
  const std::array<size_t, 5>& SizeInPacketsPerPriority() const { return size_per_priority_; }
  // Enqueue time (minus the pause time before it) of the first packet at the
  // level of `p`; MinusInfinity when that level is empty.
  Timestamp LeadingPacketEnqueueTime(PacketPriority p) const;
  // MinusInfinity when empty.
  Timestamp OldestEnqueueTime() const;
  // Mean time the queued packets have spent in the queue while unpaused, as of
  // the last UpdateAverageQueueTime (Push and SetPauseState call it).
  TimeDelta AverageQueueTime() const;
  void UpdateAverageQueueTime(Timestamp now);
  void SetPauseState(bool paused, Timestamp now);
  // RemovePacketsForSsrc for the video stream and its RTX stream, as
  // PacingController::EnqueuePacket calls it on a keyframe: the
  // retransmission, video and FEC levels.
  std::vector<PacedPacket> RemoveVideoStreamPackets();
  bool HasKeyframePackets() const { return num_keyframe_packets_ > 0; }

  static int Level(PacketPriority p);

};

class PacingController : public MetricsSource {
 public:
  explicit PacingController(PacingControllerConfig cfg = {});
  void SetPacingRates(DataRate pacing, DataRate padding);
  // Where padding comes from (PacketRouter::GeneratePadding): keep-alives, the first packet of
  // a probe and the padding rate ask it for packets, and the pacer accounts their real sizes.
  // Unset: fixed-size placeholders. A generator that returns nothing for the padding rate
  // only charges the padding budget (PacingController::ProcessPackets).
  void SetPaddingGenerator(PaddingGenerator g) { padding_generator_ = std::move(g); }
  void Enqueue(PacedPacket p);
  void AddProbeCluster(const ProbeCluster& c);
  // Sends everything that is due by `now`. Call at NextSendTime() or sooner.
  std::vector<PacedPacket> Process(Timestamp now);
  // Packets that Enqueue() discarded from the queue since the last call: the
  // first packet of a keyframe flushes the video stream's queued packets
  // (PacingController::EnqueuePacket), so the keyframe leaves as soon as it can.
  // The caller drops whatever it kept for them; they are never sent.
  std::vector<PacedPacket> TakeFlushed();
  Timestamp NextSendTime(Timestamp now) const;
  void Pause();
  void Resume();
  bool paused() const { return paused_; }
  // PacingController::SetCongested: nothing is sent while congested except a
  // keep-alive padding packet every 500 ms.
  void SetCongested(bool congested);
  bool congested() const { return congested_; }

  DataSize QueueSize() const { return queue_.SizeInBytes(); }
  size_t QueuePackets() const { return queue_.SizeInPackets(); }
  // Age of the oldest queued packet (zero when empty).
  TimeDelta OldestQueueTime(Timestamp now) const;
  // QueueSizeData() / adjusted rate.
  TimeDelta ExpectedQueueTime() const;
  // PacingController::pacing_rate(): the adjusted rate, after the long-queue boost.
  DataRate pacing_rate() const { return adjusted_media_rate_; }
  // The rate set by SetPacingRates, before the long-queue boost.
  DataRate configured_pacing_rate() const { return pacing_rate_; }
  bool probing() const { return prober_.is_probing(); }
  const PacerStats& stats() const { return stats_; }
  void ExportMetrics(MetricsWriter& w) const override;

};

}  // namespace sm::cc
```

## Verification notes

The unit tests port the intent of libwebrtc's pacing_controller_unittest.cc (burst interval, debt cap, priorities, long-queue drain on the average queue time, padding, congestion keep-alives). `PrioritizedPacketQueue` (#166) ports prioritized_packet_queue_unittest.cc: 12 of its 19 cases are ported and pass; the other 7 cover options that are off by default (audio-retransmission priority, `fast_retransmissions`, the per-level TTLs) or the round robin between SSRCs, which a one-to-one call does not need. Queue sizes are wire sizes, where libwebrtc's `SizeInPayloadBytes` counts payload and padding only (it feeds the long-queue drain rate). `PacketRouter` (#166) ports packet_router_unittest.cc against `rtp::SendSideController`, `rtp::RtpSender` and `rtp::Session`, which share the router's work: padding with no stream, RTX payload padding on the RTX SSRC, video preferred over audio for padding, RTP sequence numbers at release, and transport-wide sequence numbers. The port found two differences, now fixed: numbering starts at 1 (`transport_seq_(1)`), and a packet without the transport-cc extension gets no number and is not reported to GCC (`NotifyBweOfPacedSentPacket`); neither changes a scenario, which all map the extension. Not ported: RFC 8888 numbering (`SetGenerateTransportSequenceNumbers`), `allow_sending` and ECT(1), which are off by default; REMB and RTCP routing (`SendRemb`, `SendCombinedRtcpPacket`; the session's `RtcpEngine` sends from its one local SSRC); module registration and its death tests, `PadsOnLastActiveMediaStream`, `OnBatchComplete` and `GetRtxSsrcForMedia` between modules, which only matter with several send streams of one kind (a one-to-one call has one audio and one video stream); and `OnAbortedRetransmissions`, which M157's pacer never calls. `TaskQueuePacedSender` (#166) ports task_queue_paced_sender_unittest.cc against `Pacer` with its task-queue scheduling: 11 of its 14 cases in full and 3 in part, all passing (pacing over a second, the burst window, rescheduling on a rate change, audio sent at once, the fixed and packet-based coalescing windows, probes overriding them, probes 200 us apart, a probe ending in the send loop, the oldest wait and the queue size). The port found two differences, now fixed: the pacer's `DataSize / DataRate` and `DataSize / TimeDelta` rounded up, where libwebrtc's (api/units/data_rate.h) truncate, so a probe burst due 0.3 us past the 1 ms early-probe margin was held back (`NoMinSleepTimeWhenProbing`); and the packet-based hold back was sized by the configured pacing rate, where `MaybeProcessPackets` uses `pacing_rate()`, the rate after the long-queue boost. The first changes `issue_123_v2_allocation_min`, whose ten-seed means move both ways (4 metrics toward libwebrtc's, 5 away, all by small amounts), and `issue_48_prober_low_bandwidth` slightly; the second changes no scenario. Not ported: `RemovePacketsForSsrc` (the library removes a stream's packets only by the keyframe flush), `FirstSentPacketTime` and `ExpectedQueueTime` (not exposed by `Pacer`), and the rate change inside the `SendPacket` call stack, which `Pacer` cannot have because it returns the packets it sends. With the 40 ms burst a frame leaves in one burst, so E2E_Pacer expects that the paced run is not worse than the unpaced one on audio delay (audio is not paced in libwebrtc either; v1's case was deleted with it in #167). The pacer_v2_capacity_drop scenario keeps the v2 run reproducible; issue_5_keyframe_flush locks in the keyframe flush (docs/fixes/5-pacer-keyframe-flush.md). See docs/design/libwebrtc-parity.md 5.5 and PR #80 for the suite comparison.
