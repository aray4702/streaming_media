# 5.5 Pacer with priority queues

Section 5, *Bandwidth, congestion and unstable links*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Bursts filling router queues (bufferbloat) -> Pacer: smooths packet sends, with priority queues
- Protecting audio over video -> audio prioritized in the pacer; video degrades first

## The issue

Encoders produce a whole frame at once, and a keyframe can be tens of kilobytes. Writing it to the socket instantly sends a line-rate burst that briefly exceeds the bottleneck's capacity. Router queues fill (adding delay for everything behind the burst), packets are dropped, and the congestion controller sees self-inflicted congestion and lowers the rate for no good reason.

Audio packets queued behind a video burst arrive late, which is worse because audio dropouts hurt more than video stutter. Retransmissions are also time-critical. Packets must be spread out at a controlled rate, important traffic must go first, and the queue must never grow so large that video is seconds old when it is sent.

## Design

Encoders emit a whole frame at once; sending it as a line-rate burst fills router queues (bufferbloat) and causes self-inflicted loss. The pacer releases packets at the pacing rate (usually 2.5 x the target) and queues them per priority so audio is never stuck behind a keyframe and video degrades first. Probe clusters are sent at their own rate and tagged so the probe estimator can find them; padding fills a probe (or a requested padding rate) when there is no media to send.

Two versions sit behind one interface (`PacerConfig::version`, issue #70):

```
  v1  the library's own pacer (cc/pacer_v1.h): a media-debt leaky bucket
      with a 5 ms burst allowance; the default.
  v2  libwebrtc's PacingController and PrioritizedPacketQueue, ported as
      they are (cc/pacing_controller.h). It is not the default because its
      results are only meaningful next to the rest of libwebrtc's GoogCC
      (#61 makes it the default together with the cc v2 controller).
```

A port of modules/pacing/pacing_controller.cc, prioritized_packet_queue.cc (M157, compare/webrtc/reference/), behind a `Timestamp now` API with no task queue. Encoders emit a whole frame at once; sending it as a line-rate burst fills router queues (bufferbloat) and causes self-inflicted loss. The pacer releases packets at the pacing rate (usually 2.5 x the target) using libwebrtc's leaky bucket:

```
  - `media_debt` grows with every packet sent and drains at the (adjusted)
    pacing rate; packets may leave in a burst of `send_burst_interval`
    (40 ms) at a time, and the debt is capped at 500 ms of the rate;
  - the time that is credited since the last send is capped at 2 s;
  - a queue that cannot drain within `queue_time_limit` (2 s, judged on the
    average queue time, not the oldest packet) raises the rate;
  - the first packet of a keyframe flushes the queued packets of the video
    stream (video, FEC, retransmissions), as EnqueuePacket does;
  - padding fills a requested padding rate, 5 ms of it at a time.
```

Packets are queued per priority level, as PrioritizedPacketQueue does:

```
  0 audio, 1 retransmission, 3 video and FEC (one FIFO), 4 padding
```

(level 2, video retransmission, is only used when audio retransmission is prioritised, which libwebrtc does not do by default). Audio is not paced (it leaves at its enqueue time) but is counted in the debt, as a call with audio in the congestion-control allocation does. Probe clusters are sent at their own rate and tagged so the probe estimator can find them (#48): the BitrateProber times each burst by the cluster rate, a burst starts with a small padding packet, and padding fills a burst when there is no media.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#5-congestion-control) for the differences from libwebrtc. Two versions sit behind `PacerConfig::version` (test bench directive `pacer v1|v2`): v1, the library's own media-debt pacer (5 ms burst, drain on the oldest packet), is kept for its tests; v2, the default since the flip (#61), is a port of libwebrtc's PacingController and PrioritizedPacketQueue (M157, issue #70), judged together with the GoogCC port. v2: a 40 ms burst interval (capped at 63 kB), media debt capped at 500 ms of the rate, 2 s cap on the time credited, a queue-time limit of 2 s judged on the average queue time, audio unpaced but accounted, queue levels audio / retransmission / video+FEC / padding, keep-alive padding every 500 ms while paused or congested, and padding at the padding rate 5 ms at a time. The first packet of a keyframe flushes the queued video, FEC and retransmission packets, as `PacingController::EnqueuePacket` does (the sender marks keyframes and first-of-frame packets; #5). Remaining differences: round-robin between SSRCs of one level (one stream per kind in a one-to-one call), the packet TTLs (off by default), `SetCongested` is not driven by the congestion window (pushback stays on the encoder). `BitrateProber` (#48, `bitrate_prober.h`) times the probe bursts: a cluster starts when a media packet of at least min(`rate x min_probe_delta`, 200 B) is enqueued, begins with a small padding packet, sends `rate x min_probe_delta` bytes per burst (padding fills it when there is no media), times the next burst by the bytes sent at the cluster rate and drops a cluster whose burst is more than 10 ms late (at most 5 pending, 5 s old).

namespace sm::cc {

## Verification notes

The unit tests port the intent of libwebrtc's pacing_controller_unittest.cc (burst interval, debt cap, priorities, long-queue drain on the average queue time, padding, congestion keep-alives). With the 40 ms burst a v2 frame leaves in one burst, so the E2E_Pacer v2 case expects that the paced run is not worse than the unpaced one on audio delay (audio is not paced in libwebrtc either; v1 keeps the original expectation). The pacer_v2_capacity_drop scenario keeps the v2 run reproducible; issue_5_keyframe_flush locks in the keyframe flush (docs/fixes/5-pacer-keyframe-flush.md). See docs/design/libwebrtc-parity.md 5.5 and PR #80 for the suite comparison.
