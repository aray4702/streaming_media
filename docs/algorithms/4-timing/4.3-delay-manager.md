# 4.3 Audio target delay estimation (DelayManager)

Section 4, *Jitter, reordering and timing*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Variable packet arrival (audio) -> NetEq adaptive jitter buffer

## The issue

An audio jitter buffer must hold enough audio to absorb variation in packet arrival time, but every millisecond buffered is a millisecond of conversational delay. Above about 150 ms one-way delay, people start talking over each other. A fixed size is wrong in both directions: too small and late packets cause dropouts, too large and the call feels sluggish even on a good network.

The right size is a statistic of recent network behaviour, such as the delay that 95% of packets beat. It must rise quickly when jitter starts (or dropouts begin at once) and fall slowly (or the buffer oscillates and audio speeds up and slows down audibly). Jitter must also be measured relative to the fastest recent packet, since the clocks are not synchronized and only delay variation is observable.

## Design

Each packet's relative delay is its transit time minus the smallest transit seen in the last `history` window: how much later than the fastest recent packet it arrived. Relative delays go into a histogram with exponential forgetting; the target playout delay is the `quantile` of that histogram, so the buffer grows quickly when jitter rises and decays slowly when the network calms down.

A port of the part of NetEq that decides how much audio to buffer, used by timing::AudioJitterBuffer under `version = 2` (the test bench's `neteq v2`):

```
  - Histogram (modules/audio_coding/neteq/histogram.cc): 100 buckets in
    Q30 with a Q15 forgetting factor that starts at 0 and converges to its
    base value with start_forget_weight 2;
  - UnderrunOptimizer (underrun_optimizer.cc): the 95th percentile of the
    largest relative arrival delay in each 500 ms interval, in 20 ms
    buckets (forget factor 0.983);
  - ReorderOptimizer (reorder_optimizer.cc): the delay that minimizes
    delay + 20 ms per percent of reordered packets that would arrive too
    late (forget factor 0.9993);
  - PacketArrivalHistory (packet_arrival_history.cc): the relative arrival
    delay of a packet against the fastest packet of the last 2 s of RTP
    time, reordered packets excluded;
  - DelayManager (delay_manager.cc): max of the two optimizers, 80 ms until
    the first estimate;
  - DelayConstraints (delay_constraints.cc) and DecisionLogic::PacketArrived
    (decision_logic.cc): the minimum delay and the 75%-of-buffer cap.
```

NetEq's clock is its TickTimer, which advances 10 ms per GetAudio call; here every call takes that tick time in ms. Field-trial overrides are not ported; every default is.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#4-jitter-and-timing) for the differences from libwebrtc. A histogram with forgetting factor 0.983 per packet: rising jitter is learned within a few packets, while falling jitter takes several seconds, so the buffer does not oscillate. NetEq v2 (issue #62, `AudioJitterBufferConfig::version = 2`, the test bench's `neteq v2`; default v1): `NetEqDelayManager` ports libwebrtc M157's modules/audio_coding/neteq/ histogram.cc (Q30 buckets, Q15 forget factor from 0 with start_forget_weight 2), underrun_optimizer.cc (95th percentile of the largest relative delay of each 500 ms interval, 100 buckets of 20 ms, forget factor 0.983), reorder_optimizer.cc (forget factor 0.9993, 20 ms per percent of reordered packets lost), packet_arrival_history.cc (delay against the fastest in-order packet of the last 2 s of RTP time), delay_manager.cc (max of the two, 80 ms until the first estimate), delay_constraints.cc (minimum delay, at most 75% of a 200-packet buffer) and DecisionLogic::PacketArrived. Arrival times are on NetEq's 10 ms TickTimer, which advances once per GetAudio, and the packets before the first GetAudio are dropped from the statistics as NetEqImpl's SoftReset on the first codec does. Not ported yet (the next steps of #62): DecisionLogic's operations (accelerate above target + max arrival delay + 20 ms, preemptive expand below target, postponed decoding after an expand), BufferLevelFilter, and expand/merge timing; the v2 target drives v1's operations. NetEq::Config's max_packets_in_buffer (200) and min_delay_ms (0) are from api/neteq/neteq.h, which is not in compare/webrtc/reference/ (#57).

namespace sm::timing {

struct DelayManagerConfig {
  double quantile = 0.95;
  double forget_factor = 0.983;   // Per packet.
  int bucket_ms = 10;
  int max_delay_ms = 2000;
  int min_delay_ms = 0;

  int initial_target_ms = 60;

class DelayManager : public MetricsSource {
 public:
  explicit DelayManager(DelayManagerConfig cfg = {});

## Verification notes

No defects found. NetEq v2: libwebrtc's histogram, underrun, reorder, packet arrival history and delay manager unit tests pass. On `high_jitter` (seeds 1-10) audio concealment falls from 1.17-1.64 % to 0-0.1 % (libwebrtc 0.07-0.27 %) and the jitter buffer delay p95 rises from 72-88 ms to 104 ms (libwebrtc 100-130 ms); `issue_62_neteq_v2_high_jitter` locks it in.
