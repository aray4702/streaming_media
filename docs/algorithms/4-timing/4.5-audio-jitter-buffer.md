# 4.5 Adaptive audio jitter buffer (NetEq-lite)

Section 4, *Jitter, reordering and timing*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Variable packet arrival (audio) -> NetEq adaptive jitter buffer with time-stretching

## The issue

The audio device pulls exactly 10 ms of samples every 10 ms, at a rate the network does not control, while packets arrive late, early, out of order, duplicated or not at all. Something has to bridge the two clocks: always produce sound on time, play every packet that arrives in time, conceal the ones that do not, and keep the delay as small as the network allows.

That requires several cooperating decisions on every 10 ms pull: normal playout, concealment when the next packet is missing, merging back when it arrives, speeding up when too much is buffered, and slowing down when too little is. A naive FIFO either underruns on every jitter spike or accumulates latency forever.

## Design

Packets (decoded PCM) are inserted on arrival; the audio device pulls a fixed block (10 ms) at a time. On each pull the buffer chooses one operation:

```
  normal            next packet is present and the buffer level is on target
  accelerate        level well above target: drop one pitch period (WSOLA)
  preemptive expand level below target: add one pitch period (WSOLA)
  expand            next packet missing: synthesize audio with PLC
  merge             packet arrives after an expand: crossfade back in
```

The target delay comes from DelayManager (95th percentile of relative arrival delay), so the buffer tracks the network's jitter instead of using a fixed size. With `version = 2` (the test bench's `neteq v2`, issue #62) it comes from NetEqDelayManager, libwebrtc M157's NetEq DelayManager and its packet-arrival history, instead, and the operation is chosen as NetEq's DecisionLogic and NetEqImpl::GetDecision do (NetEqDecisionLogic): playout starts with the first packet; accelerate when the playout delay of the arrival history reaches the target plus its largest arrival delay plus 20 ms, preemptive expand below the target, at most one of them every 5 ticks; decoding is postponed after an expand while the buffer holds less than half the target; and an expand ends in a merge once it has covered the gap to the next packet. Version 2 conceals with NetEq's Expand (NetEqExpand): whole pitch periods until the sync buffer holds the output plus Expand's overlap, the mute factor that DecisionLogic reads, and Normal's unmute and 1 ms crossfade on the first decode after it. A merge (the packet after a lost one) is joined with NetEq's Merge (NetEqMerge): the decoded audio goes in where it correlates best with one more expansion, scaled to the expansion's energy and crossfaded in.

A port of libwebrtc M157's modules/audio_coding/neteq/expand.cc (with random_vector.cc and the DspHelper pieces it calls: PeakDetection, ParabolicFit, MinDistortion, CrossFade, MuteSignal and the 4 kHz downsampling filters of dsp_helper.cc). On the first call of an expand period AnalyzeSignal reads the last 256 * fs/8000 samples of the sync buffer and picks:

```
  - three pitch lags between 2.5 and 15 ms, from the correlation peaks at
    4 kHz refined by the smallest distortion (kNumCorrelationCandidates 3,
    kDistortionLength 20 * fs/8000);
  - two expansion vectors (the last lag + overlap samples, and the period
    before it scaled to the same energy when the energies are within 4x);
  - a 6th-order AR model of the unvoiced part (kLpcAnalysisLength 160 * fs/8000);
  - the voice mix factor from the correlation coefficient (the
    -5179 + 19931x - 16422x^2 + 5776x^3 polynomial above x = 0.48);
  - the mute slope from the energy ratio of the two periods, and whether
    the signal is an onset.
```

Each Process call then appends one period of `current lag` samples, the lag cycling through the three, as a voiced/unvoiced mix muted by the mute factor. The slope is at least 0.001 / (fs/8000) per sample from the 4th call and 0.002 / (fs/8000) from the 8th, and a mute factor that stops falling after the 4th call goes to 0 (Muted()). DecisionLogic reads the mute factor: decoding is postponed after an expand below 0.5.

The signal arithmetic is float on the int16 scale; the factors that drive decisions (mute factor and voice mix factor in Q14, mute slope in Q20, lags) keep libwebrtc's integer arithmetic.

NetEqBackgroundNoise ports background_noise.cc (one channel). After every GetAudio whose operation wrote decoded audio unchanged (normal, failed accelerate or preemptive expand) it reads the last 256 samples of the sync buffer. When their energy per sample is under the update threshold (500000 at start, then the lowest energy seen, rising by a factor 1 + 229/65536 per update otherwise, 4x in 4 s, and never below max energy - 60 dB) and the order-8 LPC residual is flat enough (5 * residual >= 16 * energy), it keeps the filter, the energy and the residual gain. Expand mixes the noise it generates into each period, scaled by its mute factor, which Expand sets to 0 at the start of every expand period and M157 never raises: the noise added is zero, as in libwebrtc. Its energy (2500 until the first update) sets where Normal's first decode after an expand starts its unmuting (NetEqNormalMuteFactor).

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#4-jitter-and-timing) for the differences from libwebrtc. Decoded PCM is inserted (decode-on-insert); a decoder interface that decodes at pull time (to use Opus FEC from the next packet) is a natural extension. Statistics use WebRTC getStats names. NetEq v2 (issue #62, `neteq v2`; default v1) chooses the operation as libwebrtc M157's modules/audio_coding/neteq/decision_logic.cc and NetEqImpl::GetDecision/GetAudioInternal (neteq_impl.cc) do, in `NetEqDecisionLogic` (timing/neteq_delay.h): playout starts with the first packet (new codec: a normal decode, no wait for the target); with the expected packet available and the last mode not an expand, fast accelerate at 4x and accelerate at the arrival history's playout delay >= target + its largest arrival delay + 20 ms, preemptive expand below the target, the last two at most once per kMinTimescaleInterval (5 ticks after a successful stretch, 6 after a reset); decoding is postponed while the buffer (span plus the newest packet's waiting time) is under 50 % of the target and the expand is muted below 0.5; a future packet is merged once the expand has covered the gap or the buffer is above target + 50 ms, otherwise the expand goes on; a missing packet with a later one buffered starts an expand after normal playout. The extraction rules are NetEq's: nothing is decoded while 10 ms is ready, except for merge and the stretches; accelerate needs 30 ms decoded (else normal, decoding 20 ms first); preemptive expand decodes up to 20 ms and borrows the rest of its 30 ms from the samples just played, inserting the period after them. BufferLevelFilter (buffer_level_filter.cc, Q8, factor 251-254 by target) is ported and exported as `filtered_buffer_level_samples`; M157 uses it for no decision. Differences that remain: the stretch DSP is 4.4's (correlation 0.75, segment at the block start), not accelerate.cc/preemptive_expand.cc (0.9, 15 ms); fast accelerate is a plain accelerate, as NetEq::Config::enable_fast_accelerate defaults to false (api/neteq/neteq.h, missing from compare/webrtc/reference/, #57). The v2 expand is libwebrtc M157's Expand (expand.cc, random_vector.cc and the DspHelper pieces it uses), as `NetEqExpand` (timing/neteq_expand.h): AnalyzeSignal's three lags from the 4 kHz correlation peaks and the distortion, the two expansion vectors, the order-6 AR model of the unvoiced part, the voice mix factor and the mute slope (onset above an amplitude ratio of 1.5); Process appends one lag per call, cycling through the lags, with the slope floors of the 4th and 8th calls and the stuck-gain guard, and its mute factor is what DecisionLogic's PostponeDecode reads. NetEqImpl::DoExpand's loop (whole periods until the sync buffer holds the output plus the 5/8 ms overlap), the overlap kept back as lookahead (FlushBuffers, GetAudioInternal) and Normal's first decode after an expand (one more expansion, 1 ms crossfade, unmute by at least 0.64 per 20 ms) are ported, and Expand is reset after a merge, accelerate, preemptive expand or that decode. The signal arithmetic is float on the int16 scale, so samples are not bit-exact with libwebrtc's fixed point; the factors that make decisions (mute factor, voice mix factor, mute slope, lags) keep its integer arithmetic. BackgroundNoise is ported as NetEqBackgroundNoise (background_noise.cc): after each GetAudio that left decoded audio unchanged (normal, failed accelerate or preemptive expand) it reads the last 256 samples of the sync buffer, and when their energy is under the update threshold (500000 at start, then the lowest seen, rising 4x in 4 s while the signal is louder, and kept within 60 dB of the decaying maximum) and the order-8 LPC residual is flat (5 * residual >= 16 * energy), it saves the filter, energy and gain, in libwebrtc's integer arithmetic (the Levinson-Durbin recursion in double, rounded to Q12). As in M157, the noise Expand mixes in is scaled by a mute factor that each expand period sets to 0 and nothing raises, so it adds nothing; the estimate's energy (2500 until the first save) sets where Normal's first decode after an expand starts unmuting: sqrt(noise energy / energy of its first 8 ms), or Expand's mute factor if higher. Merge is ported as NetEqMerge (merge.cc, one channel): the packet after a lost one is joined by one more unmuted expansion, repeated after the sync buffer's unplayed samples to 202 ms * fs/8000; the decoded audio goes in at the lag where its first 10 ms best correlate with that signal at 4 kHz (up to max_lag, and far enough on that the sync buffer then holds 10 ms plus the overlap and all its old samples), overwriting the unplayed expansion. It starts from the larger of Expand's mute factor and SignalScaling's sqrt(expanded / decoded energy) over 8 ms (integer arithmetic), unmutes by at least 4194 / (fs/8000) in Q20 per sample or to full gain within the frame, and is crossfaded in linearly over up to 60 * fs/8000 samples. The samples a merge adds or removes are a concealed-samples correction (StatisticsCalculator, a negative one held against later additions). The 4 kHz correlation and peak search are float (the WebRtcSpl helpers it calls, in common_audio/signal_processing, are not in compare/webrtc/reference/, #57). Still to port (issue #62): the Accelerate/PreemptiveExpand DSP, and the receive half of Opus in-band FEC.

class NetEqExpand {
 public:

  static constexpr size_t kUnvoicedLpcOrder = 6;
  static constexpr size_t kNumCorrelationCandidates = 3;
  static constexpr size_t kDistortionLength = 20;
  static constexpr size_t kLpcAnalysisLength = 160;

  // `sample_rate` is 8000, 16000, 32000 or 48000.

  // and future samples, at least history_length() of them); its last

  int consecutive_expands() const { return consecutive_expands_; }
  size_t expand_lag(int i) const { return expand_lags_[static_cast<size_t>(i)]; }

  int16_t voice_mix_factor() const { return voice_mix_factor_; }  // Q14.

  size_t last_outage_duration_samples() const { return last_outage_duration_samples_; }

// modules/audio_coding/neteq/merge.cc, one channel: the join of a decoded
// packet onto an expansion when the packet after a gap arrives (the gap was
// a loss, not a late packet). Expand runs once more, without muting, and its
// period is repeated after the sync buffer's unplayed samples to 202 ms * fs/8000.
// The decoded audio is placed at the lag where its first 10 ms correlate best
// with that signal at 4 kHz (CorrelateAndPeakSearch: at most max_lag, and far
// enough on that the sync buffer then holds 10 ms plus Expand's overlap and
// all its old samples). The decoded audio starts from the larger of Expand's
// mute factor and SignalScaling's sqrt(expanded energy / decoded energy) over
// the first 8 ms, unmuting by at least 4194 / (fs/8000) in Q20 per sample, and
// is crossfaded in linearly over min(60 * fs/8000 samples, what is left of
// the expansion, the decoded length).
class NetEqMerge {
 public:
  static constexpr size_t kMaxSampleRate = 48000;
  static constexpr size_t kExpandDownsampLength = 100;
  static constexpr size_t kInputDownsampLength = 40;
  static constexpr size_t kMaxCorrelationLength = 60;
  // GetExpandedSignal keeps at most this many unplayed samples.
  static constexpr size_t kMaxOldLength = 210 * kMaxSampleRate / 8000;

  // `sample_rate` is 8000, 16000, 32000 or 48000.
  explicit NetEqMerge(int sample_rate);

  // Merge::Process. `sync` is the sync buffer, oldest sample first, whose
  // last `future_length` samples (at least Expand's overlap) are not played
  // yet. The first old_length() samples of the merged signal overwrite the
  // unplayed ones, and the rest is appended to `out`; returns that length.
  // An unplayed part longer than kMaxOldLength is cut to its first
  // kMaxOldLength samples, with zeros put before it, as SyncBuffer's
  // InsertZerosAtIndex does.
  size_t Process(std::span<const float> input, std::vector<float>& sync, size_t future_length, NetEqExpand& expand,
                 std::vector<float>& out);
  // Merge::SignalScaling on int16 samples: sqrt(expanded / input energy) in
  // Q14 over the first min(64 * fs/8000, input_length) samples, or 16384
  // when the expansion is the louder.
  static int16_t SignalScaling(const int16_t* input, size_t input_length, const int16_t* expanded, int fs_mult);

  size_t old_length() const { return old_length_; }
  size_t best_correlation_index() const { return best_correlation_index_; }
  int16_t mute_factor() const { return mute_factor_; }  // Q14, where the decoded audio started.

};

}  // namespace sm::timing

## Verification notes

See 4.4 for the decode-ahead defect. A first comparison test was wrong: a fixed 20 ms buffer without acceleration does not conceal late packets, it waits for them, so its latency ratchets up (to 4x its target). The test now checks concealment against the loss rate and delay against both fixed buffers. NetEq v2 operations (issue #62): libwebrtc's decision_logic and buffer_level_filter unit tests pass (CNG cases excluded, no CNG here). With `neteq v2` on the whole suite (seeds 1-3), the audio gaps against libwebrtc's 10-seed means go from 112 to 94 (38 closed, 20 opened). `audio_only_lossy` jitter buffer delay p95 60 -> 72 ms and mouth-to-ear 133 -> 145 ms (libwebrtc 81 and 147); `issue_62_neteq_v2_audio_only_lossy` locks it in. NetEq v2 Expand (issue #62): expand_unittest.cc's four tests pass (DelayedPacketOutage, LostPacketOutage, CheckOutageStatsAfterReset, Muted; with a synthetic voiced signal for testfile32kHz.pcm), plus tests of the lags, the slope floors, the onset rule and the jitter buffer's expand loop. With `neteq v2` on the 19 base scenarios against libwebrtc's values on the same seeds (1-5), the audio gaps go from 46 to 42. `cross_traffic` longest audio interruption goes from 10-20 ms to 10-90 ms (libwebrtc 30-100 ms on those seeds); `issue_62_neteq_v2_cross_traffic` locks it in. NetEq v2 Merge (issue #62): merge_unittest.cc's Process test passes (input lengths 1, 2, 3, 80, 81 and 160 at 8 kHz, synthetic voiced signal), plus tests of the alignment, SignalScaling, the unmute rates, the cut of a long unplayed part and the jitter buffer's merge. With `neteq v2`, seeds 1-10: `audio_only_lossy` jitter buffer delay p95 75.0 -> 68.3 ms and mouth-to-ear p95 147.6 -> 140.9 ms (libwebrtc 81 and 146.8), time-stretch 0.24 -> 0.51 % (libwebrtc 0.39): the merge now overwrites the unplayed expansion instead of playing it before the packet. `high_jitter` and `cross_traffic` are unchanged but for `high_jitter` jitter buffer delay p50 80.4 -> 82.0 ms (libwebrtc 85).
