# 6.4 Automatic gain control and limiter

Section 6, *Audio quality (APM)*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Volume too quiet, too loud, or clipping -> AGC2 automatic gain control, limiter, clipping predictor

## The issue

Talkers differ by 30 dB or more in level: a quiet speaker far from a laptop versus someone leaning into a headset microphone. Without gain control, listeners constantly adjust their volume, quiet speakers are lost under noise, and loud ones clip, which causes harsh distortion that no later processing can undo.

Fixed gain cannot serve both. Adaptive gain must follow the speech level, not the noise (or it amplifies silence into loud hiss). It must change slowly enough not to "pump", and always guarantee that the output never exceeds full scale, even on a sudden shout.

## Design

and clipping detection.

Limiter: splits each frame into 1 ms sub-blocks, computes the gain that keeps every sub-block's peak under `ceiling_dbfs`, applies it instantly (attack) and recovers with an exponential release, interpolating the gain across each sub-block so there are no zipper artifacts. A final hard clip guarantees the ceiling.

AutomaticGainControl: estimates the speech level (RMS during VAD-active frames, slow EMA) and moves the digital gain toward target_level - speech_level, limited to `max_gain_db` and to `max_gain_change_db_per_s` so the gain never pumps. Gain is frozen in non-speech frames, and capped so the noise floor is not amplified above `max_output_noise_dbfs`. Input clipping is counted and turned into a microphone gain recommendation (the analog part of AGC1/AGC2).

adaptive digital gain, saturation protector and limiter. `apm v2` (#242).

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/agc2/ with libwebrtc's constants and defaults (agc2_common.h and AudioProcessing::Config::GainController2::AdaptiveDigital): - FixedDigitalLevelEstimator (fixed_digital_level_estimator.cc): per 10 ms

```
   frame, 20 sub-frame peak envelopes, raised one sub-frame early, instant
   attack and a 0.9971259 decay.
```

- InterpolatedGainCurve (interpolated_gain_curve.{h,cc}): the limiter's

```
   piece-wise linear gain curve, the same 32-point table (knee from
   about -0.75 dBFS, compression ratio 5 up to +1 dBFS, then saturation).
```

- Limiter (limiter.cc): per-sample gain interpolated between sub-frames,

```
   with the (1 - t)^8 attack shape on the first sub-frame, then clamped
   to the S16 range.
```

- GainApplier (gain_applier.cc): gain ramped linearly across a frame. - NoiseFloorEstimator (noise_level_estimator.cc): minimum frame energy

```
   over 5 s periods; instant decay, 0.5 attack.
```

- SpeechLevelEstimator (speech_level_estimator_impl.cc): speech-probability

```
   weighted mean of the frame RMS (dBFS) with a 400 ms time to confidence,
   committed only after 12 adjacent speech frames.
```

- SaturationProtector (saturation_protector{,_buffer}.cc): headroom from

```
   the max peak per 400 ms, delayed by a 4-slot ring buffer, clamped to
   12..25 dB, starting at 20 dB.
```

- AdaptiveDigitalGainController (adaptive_digital_gain_controller.cc):

```
   gain to bring speech level + headroom to -headroom_db (5 dB), at most
   50 dB, starting at 15 dB, capped by noise (-50 dBFS out) and by the
   limiter envelope while the level is not confident; changes at most
   6 dB/s, increases only after 12 frames with speech probability >= 0.95
   (then a 12x faster first step).
```

- GainController2 composes them for one mono 10 ms frame: fixed gain,

```
   peak/RMS levels, noise floor, speech level, saturation protector,
   adaptive digital gain, limiter.
```

Signals are FloatS16 inside (libwebrtc's scale); Process() takes and returns our [-1, 1] floats.

Differences from libwebrtc that remain (catalog.py, #242): - Speech probability comes from the caller (our Vad's per-frame

```
   decision before its hangover, as 0 or 1), not AGC2's RNN VAD: its
   weights (third_party/rnnoise) are not in the reference sources yet
   (#57).
```

- The GainController2 glue (modules/audio_processing/gain_controller2.cc)

```
   and common_audio/include/audio_util.h are not in the reference sources
   yet (#57); the order of the steps follows the components' inputs
   (AdaptiveDigitalGainController::FrameInfo) and is to be checked
   against that file once it is added.
```

- No input volume controller (AGC2's analog part is off in a call; the

```
   call default is AGC1, #242's next step).
```

the digital compression stage. `apm v2` (#242).

Every WebRTC call runs AGC1's digital stage: with the analog gain controller on (its default, `AnalogGainController::enabled`), `AudioProcessingImpl::InitializeGainController1` creates an `AgcManagerDirect`, whose `SetupDigitalGainControl` puts `GainControlImpl` in `kFixedDigital` mode with a 2 dB target, 7 dB compression gain and the limiter on; `AgcManagerDirect` then moves the compression gain between 2 and 12 dB. The desktop (`kAdaptiveAnalog`) and mobile (`kFixedDigital`) call configurations differ only in that analog volume, not here.

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/ with libwebrtc's constants and defaults: - agc/legacy/digital_agc.cc: `WebRtcAgc_CalculateGainTable` (the

```
   32-entry compressor gain table, compression ratio 3, limiter),
   `WebRtcAgc_InitDigital`, `WebRtcAgc_ComputeDigitalGains` (fast and
   slow envelope followers, the gain table lookup, the gate that lowers
   the gain without speech, the overload limit, gain reductions 1 ms
   early) and the AGC's own VAD (`WebRtcAgc_InitVad`,
   `WebRtcAgc_ProcessVad`: 4 kHz energy, short- and long-term statistics,
   log likelihood ratio).
```

- agc/legacy/analog_agc.cc: the fixed-digital parts of `WebRtcAgc_Init`,

```
   `WebRtcAgc_set_config` (the compression gain is offset by the target
   level, and the gain table's analog target is that gain) and
   `WebRtcAgc_Analyze`, and `WebRtcAgc_AddFarend`.
```

- gain_control_impl.cc: `GainControlImpl` in `kFixedDigital` mode

```
   (`ProcessCaptureAudio`, the float `ApplyDigitalGain` with 16 linear
   steps per millisecond on every band, `ProcessRenderAudio`, the
   setters and their ranges) and the configuration
   `AgcManagerDirect::SetupDigitalGainControl` gives it.
```

- common_audio/signal_processing: the fixed-point helpers these call

```
   (`WebRtcSpl_DownsampleBy2`, `WebRtcSpl_Sqrt`, `WebRtcSpl_NormU32`,
   `WebRtcSpl_NormW32`, `WebRtcSpl_DivW32W16`, `WebRtcSpl_DivW32W16ResW16`,
   `WebRtcSpl_AddSatW16`).
```

The gains and the output are bit-exact with libwebrtc's own files compiled from reference/ (golden values in `Agc1.*MatchesLibwebrtc*`).

Signals are FloatS16 inside (libwebrtc's scale); Process() takes and returns our [-1, 1] floats.

What is not here yet (catalog.py, #242): - `AgcManagerDirect` / `MonoAgc`, which picks the compression gain and

```
   the analog volume from `Agc`'s loudness histogram. `Agc` runs
   `VoiceActivityDetector` (modules/audio_processing/vad/), whose
   `VadAudioProc` needs iSAC's pitch estimator and ooura's fft4g, and
   APM's analog mic gain emulation is `CaptureLevelsAdjuster`: those
   files are not in the reference sources yet (#57). Until then the
   compression gain stays at what the caller sets (7 dB by default, as
   `SetupDigitalGainControl`).
```

- The adaptive modes' analog part (`WebRtcAgc_ProcessAnalog`,

```
   `WebRtcAgc_AddMic`, `WebRtcAgc_VirtualMic`): a call never runs it,
   because `AgcManagerDirect` always sets `kFixedDigital`.
```

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#6-audio-quality) for the differences from libwebrtc. As in AGC2, the adaptive digital gain is never negative and is capped so background noise stays under -50 dBFS; attenuation of hot inputs is left to the limiter and the mic-gain recommendation. `apm v2` (#242): `agc2::GainController2` ports libwebrtc M157's AGC2 (`modules/audio_processing/agc2/`: adaptive digital gain, speech and noise level estimators, saturation protector, limiter with its interpolated gain curve) with its defaults (headroom 5 dB, max gain 50 dB, initial gain 15 dB, 6 dB/s, noise cap -50 dBFS). Remaining differences: the speech probability is our VAD's per-frame decision, not AGC2's RNN VAD (its weights are not in the reference sources, #57), and the `GainController2` glue is to be checked against `gain_controller2.cc` once it is added (#57). AGC1, the call default: `agc1::GainControl` ports its digital compression stage, which every call runs (`AgcManagerDirect::SetupDigitalGainControl` puts `GainControlImpl` in `kFixedDigital` with a 2 dB target, 7 dB compression and the limiter): `agc/legacy/digital_agc.cc` (gain table, envelope followers, gate, overload limit, the AGC's VAD), the fixed-digital parts of `agc/legacy/analog_agc.cc` and `gain_control_impl.cc`. Not yet: `AgcManagerDirect`, which moves the compression gain (2-12 dB) and the analog volume from `Agc`'s loudness histogram; its `VoiceActivityDetector` needs iSAC's pitch estimator and ooura's fft4g, and APM's analog mic gain emulation is `CaptureLevelsAdjuster`, none of them in the reference sources yet (#57). The e2e chain still runs AGC2 for `apm v2` until AGC1 is complete.

## Verification notes

The first e2e expectations contradicted the AGC2 design (noise cap, no attenuation) and were rewritten to test those behaviours. A unit test reused a buffer that the limiter had already modified in place. AGC2 (`apm v2`, #242) on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -21.1 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC2 -21.0, v1 -16.8), loud talker -1.2 dB (-1.0), noise in pauses -62.7 dBFS (-62.9), peak 0 dBFS (0.0). libwebrtc's `GainDoesNotChangeFast` unit test never runs its loops (its frame count is negative); the port runs them. AGC1's digital stage (#242) is bit-exact with libwebrtc's `digital_agc.cc` and `analog_agc.cc` compiled from `reference/` and driven as `GainControlImpl` drives them: the gain tables for four configurations, and the 11 per-millisecond gains and the S16 output of every frame over 30 s of talk spurts at -45, -25 and -8 dBFS (`Agc1.GainTableMatchesLibwebrtc`, `Agc1.GainsAndOutputMatchLibwebrtc`). The first port used SPL's unsigned `WEBRTC_SPL_SCALEDIFF32` for the envelope followers; `digital_agc.cc` has its own signed `AGC_SCALEDIFF32`, and the golden test caught the difference.
