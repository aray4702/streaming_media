# 6.4 Automatic gain control and limiter

Section 6, *Audio quality (APM)*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Volume too quiet, too loud, or clipping -> AGC2 automatic gain control, limiter, clipping predictor

## The issue

Talkers differ by 30 dB or more in level: a quiet speaker far from a laptop versus someone leaning into a headset microphone. Without gain control, listeners constantly adjust their volume, quiet speakers are lost under noise, and loud ones clip, which causes harsh distortion that no later processing can undo.

Fixed gain cannot serve both. Adaptive gain must follow the speech level, not the noise (or it amplifies silence into loud hiss). It must change slowly enough not to "pump", and always guarantee that the output never exceeds full scale, even on a sudden shout.

## Design

and clipping detection.

Limiter: splits each frame into 1 ms sub-blocks, computes the gain that keeps every sub-block's peak under `ceiling_dbfs`, applies it instantly (attack) and recovers with an exponential release, interpolating the gain across each sub-block so there are no zipper artifacts. A final hard clip guarantees the ceiling.

AutomaticGainControl: estimates the speech level (RMS during VAD-active frames, slow EMA) and moves the digital gain toward target_level - speech_level, limited to `max_gain_db` and to `max_gain_change_db_per_s` so the gain never pumps. Gain is frozen in non-speech frames, and capped so the noise floor is not amplified above `max_output_noise_dbfs`. Input clipping is counted and turned into a microphone gain recommendation (the analog part of AGC1/AGC2).

adaptive digital gain, saturation protector and limiter. `apm v2` (#242).

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/agc2/ with libwebrtc's constants and defaults (agc2_common.h and AudioProcessing::Config::GainController2::AdaptiveDigital): - FixedDigitalLevelEstimator (fixed_digital_level_estimator.cc): per 10 ms

```
   frame, 20 sub-frame peak envelopes, raised one sub-frame early, instant
   attack and a 0.9971259 decay.
```

- InterpolatedGainCurve (interpolated_gain_curve.{h,cc}): the limiter's

```
   piece-wise linear gain curve, the same 32-point table (knee from
   about -0.75 dBFS, compression ratio 5 up to +1 dBFS, then saturation).
```

- Limiter (limiter.cc): per-sample gain interpolated between sub-frames,

```
   with the (1 - t)^8 attack shape on the first sub-frame, then clamped
   to the S16 range.
```

- GainApplier (gain_applier.cc): gain ramped linearly across a frame. - NoiseFloorEstimator (noise_level_estimator.cc): minimum frame energy

```
   over 5 s periods; instant decay, 0.5 attack.
```

- SpeechLevelEstimator (speech_level_estimator_impl.cc): speech-probability

```
   weighted mean of the frame RMS (dBFS) with a 400 ms time to confidence,
   committed only after 12 adjacent speech frames.
```

- SaturationProtector (saturation_protector{,_buffer}.cc): headroom from

```
   the max peak per 400 ms, delayed by a 4-slot ring buffer, clamped to
   12..25 dB, starting at 20 dB.
```

- AdaptiveDigitalGainController (adaptive_digital_gain_controller.cc):

```
   gain to bring speech level + headroom to -headroom_db (5 dB), at most
   50 dB, starting at 15 dB, capped by noise (-50 dBFS out) and by the
   limiter envelope while the level is not confident; changes at most
   6 dB/s, increases only after 12 frames with speech probability >= 0.95
   (then a 12x faster first step).
```

- GainController2 composes them for one mono 10 ms frame: fixed gain,

```
   peak/RMS levels, noise floor, speech level, saturation protector,
   adaptive digital gain, limiter.
```

In a call (`WebRtcVoiceEngine::ApplyOptions` on desktop: GainController1 in `kAdaptiveAnalog`, `AnalogGainController::enabled` by default), `AudioProcessingImpl` runs, per 10 ms capture frame:

```
  1. `CaptureLevelsAdjuster::ApplyPreLevelAdjustment` (with the analog mic
     gain emulation on, the capture is scaled by volume / 255 first, and
     that volume is passed to the AGC as the applied input volume);
  2. `AgcManagerDirect::AnalyzePreProcess` (clipping detection) before the
     AEC, then `Process` on split band 0 after the NS;
  3. the new compression gain, if any, into `GainControlImpl`
     (`agc1::GainControl`, agc1.h), which then applies its digital gain;
  4. `ApplyPostLevelAdjustment`, and the recommended input volume becomes
     the emulated volume of the next frame.
```

`CaptureLevels` below does steps 1 and 4; `AgcManagerDirect` step 2. The apm v2 chain (tests/e2e/audio_sims.h) wires them in this order.

The modelled mic volume: there is no device in the test bench, so the volume is APM's own emulation (`capture_level_adjustment. analog_mic_gain_emulation`, initial level 255): a linear gain of volume / 255 on the capture, ramped over the 10 ms frame when it changes. `sm_apm_compare` runs libwebrtc's APM with the same emulation.

Stack: `Process` runs `Agc`'s pitch-based VAD, whose iSAC pitch analysis keeps ~40 KB of buffers on the stack, as libwebrtc's does. On wasm (64 KB stack by default) keep large objects off the caller's stack.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#6-audio-quality) for the differences from libwebrtc. As in AGC2, the adaptive digital gain is never negative and is capped so background noise stays under -50 dBFS; attenuation of hot inputs is left to the limiter and the mic-gain recommendation. `apm v2` (#242): `agc2::GainController2` ports libwebrtc M157's AGC2 (`modules/audio_processing/agc2/`: adaptive digital gain, speech and noise level estimators, saturation protector, limiter with its interpolated gain curve) with its defaults (headroom 5 dB, max gain 50 dB, initial gain 15 dB, 6 dB/s, noise cap -50 dBFS). The `apm v2` e2e chain now runs AGC1 as a call does, in `AudioProcessingImpl`'s order. Remaining differences: the clipping predictor is not ported (off by default, so a call never creates it); AGC2's RNN VAD (#57).

## Verification notes

The first e2e expectations contradicted the AGC2 design (noise cap, no attenuation) and were rewritten to test those behaviours. A unit test reused a buffer that the limiter had already modified in place. AGC2 (`apm v2`, #242) on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -21.1 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC2 -21.0, v1 -16.8), loud talker -1.2 dB (-1.0), noise in pauses -62.7 dBFS (-62.9), peak 0 dBFS (0.0). libwebrtc's `GainDoesNotChangeFast` unit test never runs its loops (its frame count is negative); the port runs them. AGC1's digital stage (#242) is bit-exact with libwebrtc's `digital_agc.cc` and `analog_agc.cc` compiled from `reference/` and driven as `GainControlImpl` drives them: the gain tables for four configurations, and the 11 per-millisecond gains and the S16 output of every frame over 30 s of talk spurts at -45, -25 and -8 dBFS (`Agc1.GainTableMatchesLibwebrtc`, `Agc1.GainsAndOutputMatchLibwebrtc`). `Agc` and its VAD are bit-exact with the prebuilt M157 `libwebrtc.a` on x86-64 glibc (WebRtcVad decisions in four configurations, every chunkwise voice probability and RMS, and every `GetRmsErrorDb` update over 30 s; `Agc1VoiceActivity.*`); elsewhere libm may round differently, so the test checks to a tolerance there. The first goldens came from the reference files compiled with libstdc++, where fft4g's `cos(float)` is the double function; libwebrtc's libc++ picks the float overload, which the Reviewer caught and the library confirmed. The first port used SPL's unsigned `WEBRTC_SPL_SCALEDIFF32` for the envelope followers; `digital_agc.cc` has its own signed `AGC_SCALEDIFF32`, and the golden test caught the difference. `AgcManagerDirect` (#242) passes the ports of libwebrtc's `agc_manager_direct_unittest.cc` (`Agc1Manager.*`, with its mic volume and compression gain sequences, in the six parametrizations), and AGC1 in the `apm v2` chain gives on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -20.4 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC1 -20.6, v1 -16.8), loud talker -3.3 dB (-3.2), noise in pauses -62.6 dBFS (-63.0), peak 0 dBFS (0.0).
