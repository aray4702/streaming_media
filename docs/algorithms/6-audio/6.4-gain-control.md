# 6.4 Automatic gain control and limiter

Section 6, *Audio quality (APM)*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Volume too quiet, too loud, or clipping -> AGC2 automatic gain control, limiter, clipping predictor

## The issue

Talkers differ by 30 dB or more in level: a quiet speaker far from a laptop versus someone leaning into a headset microphone. Without gain control, listeners constantly adjust their volume, quiet speakers are lost under noise, and loud ones clip, which causes harsh distortion that no later processing can undo.

Fixed gain cannot serve both. Adaptive gain must follow the speech level, not the noise (or it amplifies silence into loud hiss). It must change slowly enough not to "pump", and always guarantee that the output never exceeds full scale, even on a sudden shout.

## Design

adaptive digital gain, saturation protector and limiter. `apm v2` (#242).

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/agc2/ with libwebrtc's constants and defaults (agc2_common.h and AudioProcessing::Config::GainController2::AdaptiveDigital): - FixedDigitalLevelEstimator (fixed_digital_level_estimator.cc): per 10 ms

```
   frame, 20 sub-frame peak envelopes, raised one sub-frame early, instant
   attack and a 0.9971259 decay.
```

- InterpolatedGainCurve (interpolated_gain_curve.{h,cc}): the limiter's

```
   piece-wise linear gain curve, the same 32-point table (knee from
   about -0.75 dBFS, compression ratio 5 up to +1 dBFS, then saturation).
```

- Limiter (limiter.cc): per-sample gain interpolated between sub-frames,

```
   with the (1 - t)^8 attack shape on the first sub-frame, then clamped
   to the S16 range.
```

- GainApplier (gain_applier.cc): gain ramped linearly across a frame. - NoiseFloorEstimator (noise_level_estimator.cc): minimum frame energy

```
   over 5 s periods; instant decay, 0.5 attack.
```

- SpeechLevelEstimator (speech_level_estimator_impl.cc): speech-probability

```
   weighted mean of the frame RMS (dBFS) with a 400 ms time to confidence,
   committed only after 12 adjacent speech frames.
```

- SaturationProtector (saturation_protector{,_buffer}.cc): headroom from

```
   the max peak per 400 ms, delayed by a 4-slot ring buffer, clamped to
   12..25 dB, starting at 20 dB.
```

- AdaptiveDigitalGainController (adaptive_digital_gain_controller.cc):

```
   gain to bring speech level + headroom to -headroom_db (5 dB), at most
   50 dB, starting at 15 dB, capped by noise (-50 dBFS out) and by the
   limiter envelope while the level is not confident; changes at most
   6 dB/s, increases only after 12 frames with speech probability >= 0.95
   (then a 12x faster first step).
```

- GainController2 (gain_controller2.cc) composes them for one mono 10 ms

```
   frame in its order: speech probability from AGC2's RNN VAD
   (agc2_rnn_vad.h, VoiceActivityDetectorWrapper reset every 1500 ms, as
   APM creates it with `use_internal_vad`), peak/RMS levels, noise floor,
   speech level, saturation protector, adaptive digital gain, then the
   fixed gain and the limiter. Process(frame, speech_probability) takes
   the probability from the caller instead.
```

Signals are FloatS16 inside (libwebrtc's scale); Process() takes and returns our [-1, 1] floats.

Difference from libwebrtc that remains (catalog.py, #242): no input volume controller (AGC2's analog part is off in a call; the call default is AGC1).

(`VoiceActivityDetectorWrapper` and `rnn_vad::RnnVad`), which gives `agc2::GainController2` its per-frame speech probability. `apm v2` (#242).

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/agc2/ vad_wrapper.cc and rnn_vad/, with the rnnoise weights of third_party/rnnoise/src/ (agc2_rnn_vad_weights.cc) and libwebrtc's constants: - VoiceActivityDetectorWrapper (vad_wrapper.cc): resamples the first

```
   channel of each 10 ms frame to 24 kHz (PushSincResampler, as
   PushResampler<float> does) and resets the VAD every 1500 ms
   (agc2_common.h kVadResetPeriodMs).
```

- FeaturesExtractor (features_extraction.cc): a 864-sample 24 kHz pitch

```
   buffer, the LP residual (lp_residual.cc: 5 LPC coefficients from a
   denoised auto-correlation, post-processed with 0.9^k and 0.8), the pitch
   period at 48 kHz (pitch_search*.cc: 2x decimation, the 12 kHz
   auto-correlation, two candidates, refinement at 24 kHz with
   pseudo-interpolation, the sub-harmonic search with pitch tracking) and
   the spectral features of the reference and pitch-lagged 20 ms frames
   (spectral_features*.cc: half Vorbis window, 480-point FFT, 20 Opus-scale
   triangular bands, smoothed log spectrum, 22-point DCT, cepstral
   average, derivatives, cross-correlation and variability over 8 frames).
   The HPF in front stays off, as in libwebrtc.
```

- RnnVad (rnn.cc, rnn_fc.cc, rnn_gru.cc): 42 features -> FC(24, tansig)

```
   -> GRU(24) -> FC(1, sigmoid), weights int8 / 256, rnnoise's tabulated
   activations; silence (band energy < 0.04) resets the GRU and gives 0.
```

Differences from libwebrtc that remain (catalog.py, #242): - Dot products sum in order (std::inner_product, libwebrtc's portable

```
   path); libwebrtc picks SSE2, AVX2 or NEON at run time, which sum in
   another order.
```

- The FFTs are this library's, not PFFFT (its source is not in the

```
   reference sources): a mixed-radix (2, 3, 5) DFT for the 480-point
   spectrum and sm::RealFft for the 512-point auto-correlation. Same
   transforms and scaling, rounding differs in the last bits.
```

So the speech probability matches libwebrtc's to float precision, not bit for bit.

libwebrtc M157's SincResampler and PushSincResampler: the resampler `PushResampler<float>` runs per channel, which AGC2's VAD wrapper uses to bring a 10 ms capture frame to the RNN VAD's 24 kHz (#242).

Ported from compare/webrtc/reference/common_audio/resampler/ {sinc_resampler,push_sinc_resampler}.{h,cc} with their constants: 32-tap Blackman-windowed sinc kernels at 32 sub-sample offsets (plus one), cutoff 0.9 x the lower Nyquist frequency, linear interpolation between the two kernels around the source position. The push wrapper primes the buffer on the first call with one chunk of zeros, so the delay is half a kernel.

Difference from libwebrtc: the convolution is Convolve_C, the portable path. libwebrtc picks its SSE2, AVX2 or NEON version at run time, which sum in another order (results differ in the last bits of a float).

adaptive analog controller `AgcManagerDirect` (with one `MonoAgc` per channel) and APM's capture levels adjuster with its analog mic gain emulation. `apm v2` (#242).

In a call (`WebRtcVoiceEngine::ApplyOptions` on desktop: GainController1 in `kAdaptiveAnalog`, `AnalogGainController::enabled` by default), `AudioProcessingImpl` runs, per 10 ms capture frame:

```
  1. `CaptureLevelsAdjuster::ApplyPreLevelAdjustment` (with the analog mic
     gain emulation on, the capture is scaled by volume / 255 first, and
     that volume is passed to the AGC as the applied input volume);
  2. `AgcManagerDirect::AnalyzePreProcess` (clipping detection) before the
     AEC, then `Process` on split band 0 after the NS;
  3. the new compression gain, if any, into `GainControlImpl`
     (`agc1::GainControl`, agc1.h), which then applies its digital gain;
  4. `ApplyPostLevelAdjustment`, and the recommended input volume becomes
     the emulated volume of the next frame.
```

`CaptureLevels` below does steps 1 and 4; `AgcManagerDirect` step 2. The apm v2 chain (tests/e2e/audio_sims.h) wires them in this order.

The modelled mic volume: there is no device in the test bench, so the volume is APM's own emulation (`capture_level_adjustment. analog_mic_gain_emulation`, initial level 255): a linear gain of volume / 255 on the capture, ramped over the 10 ms frame when it changes. `sm_apm_compare` runs libwebrtc's APM with the same emulation.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#6-audio-quality) for the differences from libwebrtc. The capture chain's default is `apm v2` (#565): libwebrtc's AGC1 (`AgcManagerDirect` with the emulated mic volume, as a call runs it; AGC2 on request, as libwebrtc M157's call never enables `gain_controller2`, #670) runs; the library's own AGC and limiter (v1) were retired in #565. `agc2::GainController2` (#242) ports libwebrtc M157's AGC2 (`modules/audio_processing/agc2/`: adaptive digital gain, speech and noise level estimators, saturation protector, limiter with its interpolated gain curve) with its defaults (headroom 5 dB, max gain 50 dB, initial gain 15 dB, 6 dB/s, noise cap -50 dBFS). The speech probability comes from AGC2's RNN VAD, ported in `agc2_rnn_vad.h` (`vad_wrapper.cc` and `rnn_vad/`: 24 kHz features from the LP residual's pitch and the Opus-band cepstrum, FC-GRU-FC network with the rnnoise weights, reset every 1500 ms, the capture resampled with libwebrtc's `PushSincResampler`), and `GainController2` follows `gain_controller2.cc`'s order (fixed gain after the adaptive gain, before the limiter). Its dot products sum in order (libwebrtc's portable path) and its FFTs are ours, not PFFFT, so the probability matches libwebrtc to float precision, not bit for bit. AGC1, the call default: `agc1::GainControl` ports its digital compression stage, which every call runs (`AgcManagerDirect::SetupDigitalGainControl` puts `GainControlImpl` in `kFixedDigital` with a 2 dB target, 7 dB compression and the limiter): `agc/legacy/digital_agc.cc` (gain table, envelope followers, gate, overload limit, the AGC's VAD), the fixed-digital parts of `agc/legacy/analog_agc.cc` and `gain_control_impl.cc`. `agc1_vad.h` ports `Agc`, the loudness meter `AgcManagerDirect` reads (`agc/agc.cc`, `loudness_histogram.cc`, `utility.cc`), with its `VoiceActivityDetector` (`vad/`: the WebRtcVad GMM VAD of `common_audio/vad/`, the pitch-based VAD on iSAC's pitch estimator and ooura's fft4g); it takes 16 kHz 10 ms chunks only, as `AgcManagerDirect` feeds it split band 0, so the resampler is left out. `agc_manager.h` ports `AgcManagerDirect` / `MonoAgc` (`agc/agc_manager_direct.cc`: compression gain 2-12 dB from 7 in 0.05 dB steps, the analog volume 12-255 through `kGainMap` with at most 15 dB per update, clipping handling with libwebrtc's `AnalogGainController` defaults) and APM's analog mic gain emulation (`capture_levels_adjuster/`), the test bench's modelled mic volume: the capture is scaled by volume / 255 from 255, ramped over the frame. The `apm v2` e2e chain now runs AGC1 as a call does, in `AudioProcessingImpl`'s order. `clipping_predictor.h` ports AGC1's clipping predictor (`agc2/clipping_predictor.cc`, `clipping_predictor_level_buffer.cc`: event prediction from a crest factor drop, or peak prediction with a fixed or adaptive step) and `AgcManagerDirect` asks it for a step after the clipping wait, as libwebrtc does; it is off by default (`ClippingPredictor::enabled = false`), as in libwebrtc, so a call never creates it and no metric moves.

## Verification notes

On the v1 AGC (retired in #565): the first e2e expectations contradicted the AGC2 design (noise cap, no attenuation) and were rewritten to test those behaviours, and a unit test reused a buffer that the limiter had already modified in place. AGC2 (`apm v2`, #242) on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -21.1 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC2 -21.0, v1 -16.8), loud talker -1.2 dB (-1.0), noise in pauses -62.7 dBFS (-62.9), peak 0 dBFS (0.0). libwebrtc's `GainDoesNotChangeFast` unit test never runs its loops (its frame count is negative); the port runs them. AGC1's digital stage (#242) is bit-exact with libwebrtc's `digital_agc.cc` and `analog_agc.cc` compiled from `reference/` and driven as `GainControlImpl` drives them: the gain tables for four configurations, and the 11 per-millisecond gains and the S16 output of every frame over 30 s of talk spurts at -45, -25 and -8 dBFS (`Agc1.GainTableMatchesLibwebrtc`, `Agc1.GainsAndOutputMatchLibwebrtc`). `Agc` and its VAD are bit-exact with the prebuilt M157 `libwebrtc.a` on x86-64 glibc (WebRtcVad decisions in four configurations, every chunkwise voice probability and RMS, and every `GetRmsErrorDb` update over 30 s; `Agc1VoiceActivity.*`); elsewhere libm may round differently, so the test checks to a tolerance there. The first goldens came from the reference files compiled with libstdc++, where fft4g's `cos(float)` is the double function; libwebrtc's libc++ picks the float overload, which the Reviewer caught and the library confirmed. The first port used SPL's unsigned `WEBRTC_SPL_SCALEDIFF32` for the envelope followers; `digital_agc.cc` has its own signed `AGC_SCALEDIFF32`, and the golden test caught the difference. `AgcManagerDirect` (#242) passes the ports of libwebrtc's `agc_manager_direct_unittest.cc` (`Agc1Manager.*`, with its mic volume and compression gain sequences, in the six parametrizations), and AGC1 in the `apm v2` chain gives on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -20.4 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC1 -20.6, v1 -16.8), loud talker -3.3 dB (-3.2), noise in pauses -62.6 dBFS (-63.0), peak 0 dBFS (0.0). AGC2's RNN VAD (#242) passes the ports of libwebrtc's `rnn_fc`, `rnn_gru`, `spectral_features(_internal)`, `features_extraction` and `vad_wrapper` unit tests with their test vectors and tolerances, and `PushSincResampler` the chirp error thresholds of `push_sinc_resampler_unittest.cc` (`Agc2RnnVad.*`); the tests that read libwebrtc's resource files are not ported (the files are not in the reference sources). On the test bench's speech-like signal 87% of frames reach a probability of 0.95, on white noise none. With it, AGC2 gives on the e2e quiet-then-loud talker: quiet talker -21.05 dBFS (libwebrtc AGC2 -21.0; -21.10 with our VAD's decision), loud talker -1.2 dB (-1.0), noise in pauses -62.6 dBFS (-62.9). The clipping predictor (#242) passes the ports of libwebrtc's `clipping_predictor_unittest.cc` and `clipping_predictor_level_buffer_unittest.cc` with their parametrizations (`Agc1ClippingPredictor.*`, `Agc1ClippingLevelBuffer.*`: adaptive step 17, fixed step 15), and `AgcManagerDirect` with it the predictor tests of `agc_manager_direct_unittest.cc` (used predictions lower the volume 255 -> 240 -> 225 before clipping, unused ones leave it as without the predictor); with the default config the e2e chain is unchanged.
