# 6.2 Echo-path delay estimation

Section 6, *Audio quality (APM)*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Delay between the speaker and mic paths -> echo-path delay estimation in AEC3

## The issue

Between the moment far-end audio is handed to the speaker and the moment its echo reaches the microphone there is a large, device-dependent delay: OS audio buffers, DAC and ADC, Bluetooth links. It ranges from 20 ms to over 300 ms, and it changes when the user switches from the speaker to a Bluetooth headset. An echo canceller with an 80 ms filter cannot model a 200 ms delay. If its far-end reference is misaligned it converges poorly or not at all. The delay must be measured from the audio itself, robustly against room reflections and the local talker, and re-measured when the route changes.

## Design

The far-end (render) signal reaches the microphone after the playout buffer, DAC, speaker, air and ADC: tens to hundreds of ms, and it changes when the audio route changes (speaker <-> Bluetooth). The estimator cross-correlates the last `window` of capture (Hann-windowed) against the last `max_delay + window` of render (tapered ends) using GCC-PHAT with a partial phase transform (the cross-spectrum is divided by |C|^0.8, so the peak is sharp even for coloured speech without amplifying empty bins), averages the cross-spectrum over successive updates, and reports a delay only after it has been the peak for `stable_updates` consecutive updates with a peak-to-mean confidence above a threshold.

ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/audio_processing/aec3/ {aec3_common,decimator,downsampled_render_buffer,matched_filter, matched_filter_lag_aggregator,clockdrift_detector,echo_path_delay_estimator, render_delay_controller}.{h,cc} and the low-rate half of render_delay_buffer.cc (M157), with the defaults of EchoCanceller3Config::Delay and ::RenderLevels (api/audio/ echo_canceller3_config.h of the pinned prebuilt, which is not in reference/).

```
  Decimator           6th-order elliptic low-pass (factor 4) or a 5x
                      Chebyshev band-pass (factor 8), then a 1 kHz high-pass
                      (factor 4 only), then every 4th/8th sample: 64-sample
                      16 kHz blocks become 16 (or 8) samples at 4 (2) kHz.
  MatchedFilter       5 NLMS filters of 32 sub-blocks (512 taps at 4 kHz,
                      128 ms each) at shifts of 24 sub-blocks, so together
                      they cover 0-552 ms. Step 0.7, updated only when the
                      render energy over the window exceeds 150^2 per tap and
                      the capture isn't saturated. A filter's peak is a lag
                      candidate when it is away from the filter ends and its
                      error is below 0.2 of the capture energy; the filter
                      with the lowest error wins. Pre-echo: the accumulated
                      error per 4 taps finds an earlier part of the echo.
  MatchedFilterLagAggregator  250-entry histogram of winning lags (minus
                      the 32-sample headroom, 8 at 4 kHz); reports a coarse
                      delay once a lag has more than 5 votes and a refined
                      one once it has more than 20. With pre-echo detection
                      on (the default) the reported delay is the pre-echo
                      candidate, in blocks of 16 samples at 4 kHz.
  EchoPathDelayEstimator  decimates the capture, runs the filters (slow
                      smoothing, also 0.7, once a refined delay was found),
                      aggregates, feeds the clock-drift detector, and soft
                      resets the filters after 125 identical estimates.
  RenderDelayController  turns the estimate (samples at 16 kHz) into a
                      render buffer delay in 64-sample blocks with a
                      hysteresis of 1 block once both estimates are refined.
  RenderDelayBufferLowRate  the decimated render ring that the filters read:
                      newest sample first, read index one sub-block ahead of
                      write after a reset, overrun/underrun and excess-render
                      detection (250 blocks, more than 8 blocks ahead).
```

Several capture channels are made mono by the capture AlignmentMixer (adaptive selection by default), as echo_path_delay_estimator.cc does. Differences that remain (tools/catalog.py 6.2): the portable C MatchedFilterCore, where x86 libwebrtc runs the SSE2/AVX2 variants (same arithmetic, different float summation order); no RenderDelayControllerMetrics (UMA histograms only) or data dumps. The blocks, spectra and FFTs of RenderDelayBuffer and the delay it applies to them come with the adaptive filter (#240's next step); nothing calls this module in a call yet.

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#6-audio-quality) for the differences from libwebrtc. The capture chain's default is `apm v2` (#565): AEC3's own delay estimation (`audio/aec3_delay.h`) runs, and this GCC-PHAT estimator runs only with `apm_v2 = false` until it is retired. The search range is 500 ms; updates run every 100 ms and only while the far end is active. AEC3's delay estimation (issue #240 step 1, `audio/aec3_delay.h`, for `apm v2`) is a port of libwebrtc M157's decimator, matched filter, lag aggregator, clock-drift detector, echo-path delay estimator, render delay controller and the low-rate half of the render delay buffer, with EchoCanceller3Config's delay defaults (5 filters of 32 sub-blocks at 4 kHz covering 0-552 ms, headroom 32 samples, thresholds 5/20, pre-echo detection on), and `alignment_mixer`, which makes several render and capture channels mono for it (adaptive selection of the strongest channel, M157's default; #565). It runs inside EchoCanceller3. Remaining differences: the portable C matched-filter core where x86 libwebrtc runs SSE2/AVX2 (float summation order only); no UMA metrics or data dumps.

## Verification notes

Defect found: with rectangular windows, the window edges correlated with each other and produced false peaks at both ends of the lag range (0 and 8000 samples). Fixed with Hann/Tukey tapering and a partial phase transform (|C|^0.8). The synthetic room response was also made physical (direct path dominant). AEC3 delay estimation (#240): the mono cases of libwebrtc's decimator, matched_filter, matched_filter_lag_aggregator, clockdrift_detector and echo_path_delay_estimator unit tests pass (both down-sampling factors, with and without pre-echo detection; the SIMD-equivalence and death tests don't apply). On speech through a room response with an echo path of 60 then 180 ms, the render delay controller reports 832 and 2752 samples (true 968 and 2888 minus the 32-sample headroom, one pre-echo block early) with a refined estimate and no clock drift; the first estimate comes after 176 ms.
