# 7.8 QP-based quality scaler

Section 7, *Video quality and processing*, of [Problems WebRTC solves](../../02-webrtc-problems-solved.md). Problems addressed:

- Region-of-interest quality -> quality scaler and per-codec QP control

## The issue

The bandwidth estimate alone does not tell the sender whether its resolution is right. Content complexity matters: a talking head at 300 kbps looks fine at 640x360, while a busy scene at the same bitrate falls apart. The encoder reports how hard it is quantizing (QP). A consistently high QP means the picture is blocky and a lower resolution would look better; a consistently low QP means bits are to spare and resolution could rise. QP is noisy from frame to frame, its scale differs per codec, and decisions must not oscillate.

## Design

When the bitrate is too low for the resolution the encoder raises its quantizer (QP) and the picture turns blocky; a smaller resolution at the same bitrate looks better. The scaler averages the QP of encoded frames (exponential filter) and, every `sampling_period`:

```
  avg QP > high threshold, or > 60% frames dropped  -> adapt down
  avg QP < low threshold                             -> adapt up
```

Thresholds are per codec (libwebrtc defaults): VP8 29/95 (0..127), VP9 120/180 and AV1 145/205 (0..255), H.264 24/37 (0..51). After a resolution change the filter is reset so the next decision uses QP measured at the new resolution. Region-of-interest QP (lower QP on faces or text) is an encoder-side refinement on top of these thresholds.

`version = 2` is libwebrtc M157's QualityScaler (#84 step 2), ported from compare/webrtc/reference/modules/video_coding/utility/quality_scaler.cc with its constants: the first check 2 s (`kMeasureMs`) after the first Check() call (libwebrtc starts its task at construction), then every 2 s while `fast_rampup_` (until the first adapt-down) and every 2 s x 2.5 (`kSamplePeriodScaleFactor`) = 5 s after it; 1 s while the previous check saw too few frames. A check needs 60 frames (`kMinFramesNeededToScale`) over a 150-frame window (`5 * 30`), encoded or dropped. It reports high QP when the drop rate over that window is >= 60% (`kFramedropPercentThreshold`, rounded down) or the smoothed QP is > high, and low QP when it is <= low; every report clears the samples. QP is smoothed as QualityScalingExperiment does by default (it is on unless the `WebRTC-Video-QualityScaling` trial says `Disabled`): two rtc::ExpFilter-style filters over the sample times in ms, alpha 0.9995 for the high check and 0.9999 for the low check, read as int, with drops of every reason counted. The experiment's source is not in reference/ (#57); sm_video_compare matches libwebrtc's binary on every quality-scaler row with these values (docs/results/video-webrtc.md).

VideoStreamAdapter is libwebrtc's VideoStreamAdapter, ported from compare/webrtc/reference/call/adaptation/video_stream_adapter.cc and video_source_restrictions.cc (the reference files of #302), with every degradation preference (issue #646):

```
  - MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE, resolution steps:
    - adapt down (DecreaseResolution): max_pixels_per_frame = 3/5 of the
      current input frame's pixels (GetLowerResolutionThan), target unset;
      refused (kLimitReached) when that is not below the current maximum or
      falls under min_pixels_per_frame (EncoderInfo::ScalingSettings,
      kDefaultMinPixelsPerFrame = 320 x 180);
    - adapt up (IncreaseResolution): target = 5/3 of the input pixels
      (GetHigherResolutionThan), or unrestricted when this undoes the last
      step; max_pixels_per_frame = 12/5 of the target
      (GetIncreasedMaxPixelsWanted); refused when that is not above the
      current maximum;
    - after a step it waits for the input frame size to change before
      taking another in the same direction (awaiting_frame_size_change_;
      only in MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE).
  - MAINTAIN_RESOLUTION, frame-rate steps: max_frame_rate = 2/3 of the
    input fps down (GetLowerFrameRateThan, at least kMinFrameRateFps = 2),
    3/2 up (GetHigherFrameRateThan), unrestricted when this undoes the last
    step; it needs an input of at least 2 fps (HasSufficientInputForAdaptation).
  - BALANCED: down, the frame rate goes to BalancedDegradationSettings'
    MinFps for the frame size first, then the resolution as
    MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE; a frame-rate step that leaves less than MinFpsDiff
    below the input fps takes one more step at once
    (AdaptIfFpsDiffInsufficient). Up, the frame rate goes to MaxFps first
    (the next config's fps), then the resolution; BalancedConstraint
    refuses a step up below the configs' kbps / kbps_res for the encoder
    target.
  - MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE_AND_RESOLUTION: kAdaptationDisabled.
  - SetDegradationPreference to or from BALANCED clears the restrictions;
    the restrictions the source sees are filtered by the preference
    (FilterRestrictionsByDegradationPreference).
```

GetAdaptationDown / GetAdaptationUp compute a step without applying it and ApplyAdaptation applies one, as libwebrtc's Adaptation objects do, so the resource bookkeeping (rtp::VideoSendController's port of ResourceAdaptationProcessor) can record a step it does not apply. GetAdaptDownResolution is the step the initial frame drop and the bandwidth quality scaler take (one forced resolution step in BALANCED). The camera call uses MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE (#635), which VideoStreamEncoder sets before any adaptation, so that is this adapter's default.

BitrateConstraint (video/adaptation/bitrate_constraint.cc, issue #819), for every preference: a step up that raises the resolution is refused (kRejectedByConstraint) while the encoder target is below the min_start_bitrate_bps of the encoder's ResolutionBitrateLimits for GetHigherResolutionThan(current frame size) (EncoderInfo::GetEncoderBitrateLimitsForResolution: the smallest limit at or above that size). With no limits (libwebrtc's software VP8, VP9, H.264 and AV1 encoders report none by default, nor does the test bench's encoder model or libwebrtc's in sm_compare), an unknown or zero target, or no frame yet, every step is allowed, as there. The current frame size stands for VideoStreamInputState::single_active_stream_pixels, the one configured layer's size; simulcast and SVC, for which libwebrtc skips the limits, are not ported: a one-to-one call sends one layer.

BalancedDegradationSettings is rtc_base/experiments/balanced_degradation_settings.cc with its default configs (320x240: 7 fps; 480x360: 10 fps, fps_diff 1; 640x480: 15 fps, fps_diff 1). The field trial WebRTC-Video-BalancedDegradationSettings is not parsed; a config list can be passed instead and is validated as the trial's (IsValid, else the defaults).

VideoAdapter is libwebrtc's api/video/video_adapter.cc AdaptFrameResolution for one input size and the sink's max/target pixel counts (no output format request, no aspect-ratio crop, no frame-rate limit): FindScale alternates 3/4 and 2/3 (starting with 2/3 when both sides divide by 3) and picks the scale closest to the target that is not above the maximum, then rounds the crop to the scale's denominator times the alignment. VideoStreamEncoder's VideoSourceSinkController passes the restrictions as the sink wants (max_pixel_count, target_pixel_count).

See the [libwebrtc parity inventory](../../design/libwebrtc-parity.md#7-video-quality-and-processing) for the differences from libwebrtc. Per-region QP (ROI) is an encoder-side feature built on top of these thresholds.

## Verification notes

An expectation was wrong: at 300 kbps, 640x360 already gives QP 33, which is inside the H.264 band, so not going lower is correct. The test now checks that QP returns inside the band. The test bench and `sm_compare` share an encoder model for this module's inputs (`testbench/encoder_model.h`, issue #84 step 1): QP 30 at libwebrtc's per-resolution start rates (720p 1000 kbps, 360p 500 kbps), +6 per halving of the bits per frame, clamped to 0..51, and 10 ms per 720p frame of encode time. Deviation: a model in place of a codec, identical on both stacks, so scaling decisions compare like for like. `version = 2` (issue #84 step 2; the default since #579) is libwebrtc M157's `QualityScaler` (`modules/video_coding/utility/quality_scaler.cc`) with `QualityScalingExperiment`'s default smoothers: 2 s checks until the first adapt-down, then 5 s; 60 frames over a 150-frame window; drop rate >= 60%; smoothed QP > high or <= low. `sm_video_compare` gives the same signals as libwebrtc on all six quality-scaler inputs ([results](../../results/video-webrtc.md)); the unit tests port `quality_scaler_unittest.cc`. Issue #84 step 4 wires version 2 into the send path behind `quality_scaling` (`rtp::VideoSendController`, scenario key `quality_scaling on`, off by default): libwebrtc's `QualityScalerResource` with `MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE`, `VideoStreamAdapter`'s resolution steps (3/5 down, 5/3 up with a 12/5 maximum, the 320x180 minimum, waiting for the frame size to change), `VideoAdapter`'s 3/4-2/3 scale series and `VideoStreamEncoder::DropDueToSize` (up to 4 initial frames dropped as too large for the target, each adapting down), from `call/adaptation/` and `api/video/video_adapter.cc` (reference files of #302). `sm_compare` runs libwebrtc with the same settings (`ScalingSettings(24, 37)`, `MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE`, its own `VideoAdapter` in the source, and `QualityScalingExperiment`'s default string, whose H.264 slot is 24/37; the model encoder reports H.264 since #339). A CheckQpTask due at a frame's capture time runs after that frame was encoded, and its restriction reaches the source through a worker-queue task (`VideoStreamEncoder::OnVideoSourceRestrictionsUpdated`), so a step applies from the next frame; verified on yardstick #364, where both stacks then give the same checks, steps and freezes on `issue_23_outage_500ms` with scaling on ([fix #348](https://github.com/aray4702/streaming_media/issues/348)). Step 5 adds the CPU resource and `ResourceAdaptationProcessor`'s per-resource bookkeeping (`call/adaptation/resource_adaptation_processor.cc`): each resource keeps the restrictions it last asked for, and an adapt-up applies only for the resource with the most adaptations, alone (`kNotMostLimitedResource`, `kSharedMostLimitedResource`); it is `video::ResourceAdaptationProcessor`, ported with the libwebrtc unit tests (#646). #646 also ports the removal of a resource, as `VideoStreamEncoderResourceManager` does on an encoder info change (`VideoSendController::OnEncoderInfoChanged`; `ConfigureQualityScaler`, `ConfigureEncodeUsageResource`): the quality scaler stops without trusted QP thresholds (and the initial frame drop with it, restarted when it is added again), the CPU check without `enable_cpu_overuse_detection` (a started one restarts); `RemoveLimitationsImposedByResource` clears the restrictions when the removed resource was the only adapted one, goes back to the most limited remaining resource's restrictions and counters when it was more limited (`VideoStreamAdapter::GetAdaptationTo`), and the removed resource's later signals are ignored. The test bench's encoder never changes its info, so no scenario moves. #646 ports `VideoStreamAdapter`'s other degradation preferences: MAINTAIN_RESOLUTION's frame-rate steps (2/3 down, 3/2 up, at least 2 fps), BALANCED (frame rate to `BalancedDegradationSettings`' min fps for the frame size first, then resolution; up, the next config's fps first; `AdaptIfFpsDiffInsufficient`; `BalancedConstraint`'s kbps limits; `GetAdaptDownResolution`), MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE_AND_RESOLUTION (disabled), the clear on a switch to or from BALANCED and the restrictions filtered by the preference, with `balanced_degradation_settings.cc`'s default configs (320x240: 7 fps, 480x360: 10 fps, 640x480: 15 fps), ported with the libwebrtc unit tests; the camera call stays MAINTAIN_FRAMERATE (#635), so no scenario moves. #646 also ports the initial-drop reset on a start-bitrate drop (`InitialFrameDropper::SetTargetBitrate`, behind QualityScalerSettings' `initial_bitrate_interval_ms` / `initial_bitrate_factor`, unset by default as in M157) and `pending_frame_`: a frame dropped for its size or a pause is held, the next frame releases it (kEncoderQueue), and a resume encodes it at once if it fits and is under 1 s old (`OnBitrateUpdated`, `kPendingFrameTimeoutMs`); unit tests cover each rule, and no scenario pauses the encoder on either stack (the parity traces show only the 4 initial drops). The drop checks run in `VideoStreamEncoder`'s order when quality scaling is on: the congestion-window drop (`OnFrame`), then `DropDueToSize` and `OnMaybeEncodeFrame`, the outage pause (`EncoderPaused`) and the frame dropper. `issue_84_quality_scaling_720p_low_rate` and `issue_84_quality_scaling_rate_rise` lock in the behaviour.
